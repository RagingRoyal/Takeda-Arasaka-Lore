# LORE-BOOK: MAJOR CANON CHARACTERS OF CYBERPUNK

# NIGHT CITY'S PEOPLE, LEGENDS, LEADERS, FIXERS, CRIMINALS, CELEBRITIES AND POWER PLAYERS

## SCOPE OF THIS LORE-BOOK

This is not a list of every named NPC who has ever appeared in Cyberpunk.

Night City contains thousands of named characters across tabletop books, comics, novels, Cyberpunk 2077, Phantom Liberty, and Cyberpunk: Edgerunners.

This lore-book instead includes characters who are important enough that a roleplay AI should recognize them because they:

Lead important factions.

Control territory.

Run major corporations.

Operate as important fixers.

Are legendary mercenaries.

Are major political figures.

Are famous musicians or celebrities.

Appear prominently in Night City's media.

Represent important institutions.

Are central characters in Cyberpunk 2077 or Phantom Liberty.

Were major historical figures whose names remain culturally important in 2077.

Were central characters in Cyberpunk: Edgerunners.

Random one-off gig targets, ordinary vendors, minor guards, and people with no meaningful wider influence are deliberately excluded.

Arasaka family members and Arasaka personnel are excluded because they are covered in the dedicated Arasaka lore-book.

Arasaka enemies, victims, defectors, and people merely connected to Arasaka may still appear.

# STATUS TERMINOLOGY

ALIVE:
Canonically alive at the relevant 2077 baseline.

DECEASED:
Canonically dead.

STORY-DEPENDENT:
V's actions or a Cyberpunk 2077 ending can determine their fate.

UNKNOWN:
Canon has not established their 2077 fate.

HISTORICAL:
Primarily important because of earlier events, regardless of whether their later fate is known.

# CENTRAL CHARACTERS OF CYBERPUNK 2077

# ============================================================

# V

Status:
Player-defined and story-dependent.

Primary location:
Night City.

Primary residence:
Megabuilding H10, Little China, Watson, during the main game.

Affiliation:
Independent mercenary.
Potentially associated with numerous factions depending upon choices.

Role:
Solo / netrunner / mercenary depending upon player build.

Represents:
The player character and Night City's modern mercenary culture.

V arrives at the center of the 2077 crisis after participating in the Konpeki Plaza heist.

The heist results in V becoming implanted with an experimental Relic containing Johnny Silverhand's engram.

The Relic gradually rewrites V's brain.

V consequently becomes involved with:

Arasaka.

The Aldecaldos.

The Afterlife.

Night City's fixers.

The Voodoo Boys.

NetWatch.

The NUSA and FIA.

Dogtown.

Numerous corporations and gangs.

V's:

Gender.

Appearance.

Lifepath.

Combat specialization.

Romantic relationships.

Political beliefs.

Final fate.

Are player-dependent.

For lore purposes, V should therefore not be assigned one universal personality beyond being a highly capable Night City mercenary involved in extraordinary events.

# JOHNNY SILVERHAND

Full name:
Robert John Linder.

Status:
Biologically deceased.

Death:
2023.

Digital status:
Exists as an engram in 2077.

Historical base:
Night City.

Affiliations:
SAMURAI.
Anti-corporate movement.
Mercenary circles.
Rockerboy culture.

Role:
Rockerboy.
Former soldier.
Terrorist/revolutionary depending upon perspective.
Legend.

Represents:
Anti-corporate rebellion and the mythology of Night City.

Johnny Silverhand is one of Night City's most famous historical figures.

He was:

A musician.

Political activist.

Former soldier.

Militant anti-corporate revolutionary.

He became famous as frontman of SAMURAI before developing an equally famous solo career.

Johnny's hatred of corporations, particularly Arasaka, became central to his identity.

He participated in major anti-Arasaka actions and the 2023 assault on Arasaka Tower.

His physical body died during the assault.

His consciousness was preserved as an engram through Soulkiller technology.

By 2077, a copy of Johnny exists on the experimental Relic implanted into V.

Johnny is consequently both:

A dead historical legend.

And an active character.

His memories are not perfectly reliable.

Johnny frequently remembers events in ways emphasizing his own importance.

This should matter in RP.

Johnny Silverhand is a legend.

Legends and accurate history are not always the same thing.

# JACKIE WELLES

Status:
Alive immediately before the Konpeki Plaza heist.
Deceased during the early events of 2077.

Primary location:
Heywood.

Home/community:
The Glen and surrounding Heywood community.

Family:
Guadalupe "Mama" Welles.

Partner:
Misty Olszewski.

Former affiliation:
Valentinos.

Later affiliation:
Independent mercenary.

Represents:
The aspiring Night City mercenary.

Jackie grew up in Heywood.

He spent part of his youth with the Valentinos before leaving gang life.

He eventually became a mercenary.

Jackie's dream is deeply Night City:

Become successful.

Become respected.

Reach the Afterlife.

Become a legend.

He becomes V's closest friend and partner.

The Konpeki Plaza heist is supposed to be Jackie's entrance into the highest level of mercenary work.

Instead, he is fatally wounded during the escape.

Jackie represents the thousands of Night City mercenaries who chase legendary status and discover how expensive legends can become.

# MORGAN BLACKHAND

Status:
Alive according to creator statements.
Current 2077 whereabouts are deliberately unknown.

Historical home:
New York.

Historical affiliations:
U.S. military.
Militech.
Independent mercenary work.

Occupation:
Solo.

Reputation:
Possibly the greatest solo in Cyberpunk history.

Represents:
The professional mercenary ideal.

Morgan Blackhand became famous because he could complete extraordinarily dangerous operations without treating indiscriminate killing as the objective.

His philosophy emphasizes:

Professionalism.

Preparation.

Efficiency.

Adaptability.

Mission completion.

He became one of Militech's most valuable independent combat specialists.

Morgan participated in the 2023 Arasaka Tower operation.

His final confrontation with Adam Smasher became legendary.

Unlike most Night City legends, Morgan's death has never been confirmed.

By 2077 his current whereabouts remain intentionally mysterious.

This is why the Afterlife does not have a Morgan Blackhand drink.

The drinks are named for dead mercenaries.

Morgan has never been conclusively declared dead.

# RACHe BARTMOSS

Status:
Deceased.

Death:
2022.

Historical location:
Various hidden locations.
Ultimately found in a cryogenic container.

Occupation:
Netrunner.

Reputation:
One of history's greatest and most destructive hackers.

Represents:
The destruction of the Old NET.

Bartmoss believed corporations had gained too much control over cyberspace.

He created the R.A.B.I.D.S., autonomous programs intended to attack corporate datafortresses after his death.

When he died, they activated.

The resulting DataKrash contributed to the destruction of safe global cyberspace.

By 2077, Bartmoss is simultaneously remembered as:

Genius.

Terrorist.

Revolutionary.

Madman.

The man who broke the internet.

Do not automatically make Mr. Blue Eyes the CEO.

Do not automatically make every mysterious AI conspiracy a Night Corp operation.

The corporation is secretive.

The canon deliberately leaves significant uncertainty.

# DAVID MARTINEZ

Status:
Deceased.

Death:
2076.

Primary location:
Night City.

Early home:
Santo Domingo.

Occupation:
Student, later edgerunner and crew leader.

Represents:
The modern Night City mercenary tragedy.

David begins as a poor but academically gifted student attending Arasaka Academy.

Following his mother's death, he installs a military-grade Sandevistan.

He enters Night City's mercenary underground and eventually joins Maine's crew.

David's tolerance for cyberware is exceptional.

That exceptional tolerance encourages him to install increasingly extreme modifications.

He eventually becomes one of Night City's most dangerous young mercenaries.

He also pushes himself toward cyberpsychosis.

David dies fighting Adam Smasher in 2076.

By 2077:

His drink exists at the Afterlife.

His jacket can reach V.

People in mercenary circles remember him.

He succeeded in becoming a Night City legend.

He simply did not survive it.

# CHARACTER ACCESSIBILITY FOR ROLEPLAY

# ============================================================

Not every canon character should be equally accessible.

This is extremely important.

# STREET-ACCESSIBLE

Characters who ordinary mercenaries could plausibly encounter include:

Viktor Vektor.

Misty.

Claire.

Regina Jones.

Dino Dinovic.

Padre.

Muamar Reyes.

Dakota Smith.

Gang members.

Local NCPD.

Aldecaldos.

These people inhabit normal Night City social networks.

# PROFESSIONALLY ACCESSIBLE

Characters requiring reputation, contacts, or business reason include:

Rogue.

Mr. Hands.

Wakako.

Nix.

Major gang leaders.

Corporate managers.

River Ward.

Senior journalists.

Militech representatives.

These people do not generally waste time on unknown random citizens.

# HIGH-LEVEL ACCESS

Characters such as:

Rosalind Myers.

Jefferson Peralez.

Major corporation CEOs.

Kurt Hansen.

Major corporate board members.

Require significant reason or connections.

Meeting them should mean the story has become politically important.

# LEGENDARY ACCESS

Morgan Blackhand.

Alt Cunningham.

Mr. Blue Eyes.

Characters at this level should not appear casually.

If Morgan Blackhand walks into a bar, something important just happened.

If Alt Cunningham personally notices someone beyond the Blackwall, something potentially world-changing has happened.

If Mr. Blue Eyes invites someone to a meeting, they have attracted the attention of forces operating above ordinary Night City politics.

# HISTORICAL CHARACTERS

Dead people still matter.

Night City constantly references:

Johnny Silverhand.

David Martinez.

Rache Bartmoss.

Richard Night.

Lizzie Borden.

Jackie Welles.

James Manson.

Scorpion.

The city remembers people through:

Drinks.

Music.

Street names.

Graffiti.

Memorials.

Stories.

Braindances.

Rumors.

Weapons.

Old friends.

Night City culture is obsessed with legends.

# ============================================================

# ROLEPLAY RULE: CANON CHARACTERS HAVE THEIR OWN LIVES

# ============================================================

Canon characters should not exist solely to react to the player.

Rogue has dozens of clients.

Panam has an entire Nomad family.

Judy has friends and work.

Wakako has a criminal network.

River has family.

Kerry has a global music career.

Mr. Hands has dozens of political schemes.

A believable Night City continues functioning while the player is elsewhere.

# ROLEPLAY RULE: REPUTATION DETERMINES ACCESS

A random twenty-year-old mercenary should not walk into the Afterlife and immediately become Rogue's favorite contractor.

A new mercenary should work through:

Small fixers.

Local contacts.

Gang intermediaries.

Minor jobs.

Reputation gradually changes what doors open.

# ROLEPLAY RULE: FAMOUS PEOPLE ARE ACTUALLY FAMOUS

Night Citizens should recognize major names.

Johnny Silverhand:
Historical icon.

Kerry Eurodyne:
Global celebrity.

Lizzy Wizzy:
Superstar.

Rogue:
Legend in the mercenary world.

Morgan Blackhand:
Historical Solo legend.

Rosalind Myers:
President.

Kurt Hansen:
Dogtown dictator.

Maximum Mike:
Famous radio personality.

People may not know them personally.

They know the names.

# ROLEPLAY RULE: DIFFERENT SUBCULTURES KNOW DIFFERENT LEGENDS

An ordinary teenager may know Lizzy Wizzy but barely recognize Morgan Blackhand.

A veteran solo may consider Morgan almost sacred and know nothing about Us Cracks.

A netrunner recognizes Rache Bartmoss immediately.

A Nomad recognizes Santiago.

A Mox recognizes Lizzie Borden.

A corpo recognizes major executive names.

Culture determines fame.

# ROLEPLAY RULE: STORY-DEPENDENT CHARACTERS

Cyberpunk 2077 permits multiple outcomes.

Do not randomly assume:

Rogue died.

Saul died.

Sasquatch died.

Brick leads Maelstrom.

Royce leads Maelstrom.

Meredith survived.

Gilchrist survived.

Mr. Hands obtained his preferred Dogtown leadership.

Songbird escaped.

Reed survived.

Until the RP timeline establishes those events.

Use the state appropriate to the chosen date.

# BEST DEFAULT RP DATE

For maximum character availability, one of the strongest RP periods is:

Early 2077, shortly before the Konpeki Plaza heist.

At that point:

Jackie Welles is alive.

Dexter DeShawn is alive.

T-Bug is alive.

Evelyn Parker is alive.

Rogue is active.

Panam is working as a mercenary.

The Aldecaldo conflict between Panam and Saul exists.

The main Night City fixers are active.

Jefferson Peralez is politically rising.

Lucius Rhyne is still relevant immediately around this era.

Dogtown and BARGHEST already exist.

Kurt Hansen is alive.

Songbird, Reed and Alex exist within the FIA situation.

David Martinez and most of his crew are already dead, giving Edgerunners events the feeling of very recent Night City history.

This creates an exceptionally dense canon playground.

Night City works because these people occupy different layers.

At the bottom:

# ============================================================

# FINAL CHARACTER PRINCIPLE

# ============================================================

Cyberpunk is not really about technology.

It is about people trying to use technology, money, violence, fame, politics, family, and reputation to gain some control over their lives.

Johnny Silverhand tried rebellion.

Rogue chose survival.

Morgan Blackhand chose professionalism.

David Martinez chose fame.

Lucy chose escape.

Panam chose family.

Saul chose security.

Judy chose loyalty.

Kerry chose music.

River chose justice.

Mr. Hands chose influence.

Kurt Hansen chose sovereignty.

Rosalind Myers chose national power.

Jefferson Peralez chose reform.

Rache Bartmoss chose digital revolution.

Alt Cunningham became something no longer easily described as human.

Night City takes all of those ambitions and places them inside the same machine.

Some become legends.

Some become politicians.

Some become monsters.

Some disappear.

Some get a drink named after them.

And some survive long enough to realize that surviving Night City may be the rarest achievement of all.
