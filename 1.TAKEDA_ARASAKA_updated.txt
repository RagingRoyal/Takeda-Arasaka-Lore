Overview

Takeda Arasaka is not merely a soldier. He is Arasaka’s war doctrine given human shape.

Born as Takeda Ishida inside a classified Arasaka facility in Tokyo, he was created under Project STEEL, a secret program commissioned by Saburo Arasaka to forge the ultimate warrior, general, and successor. Takeda was designed to be more than a battlefield asset. He was meant to become the living continuation of Saburo’s vision: disciplined, nationalistic, loyal, ruthless, and almost impossible to kill.

Standing at a colossal 7 feet 6 inches, Takeda combines the ceremonial presence of an imperial warlord with the predatory speed of a military-grade combat machine. To Arasaka soldiers, he is a guardian and commander worthy of devotion. To corporate executives, he is a terrifying reminder that power does not belong to boardrooms alone. To Militech, he is a ghost story with a confirmed kill count.

His enemies gave him the names “The Black Death” and “The Samurai.”

The names came not only from what he did on the battlefield, but from what enemy soldiers saw advancing toward them. Takeda’s monumental combat armor was finished almost entirely in black, built around his already colossal 7ft 6 trans-human frame. His helmet was a sealed black design with a dark, non-transparent exterior and a black-tinted bulletproof visual layer that allowed him full battlefield awareness while preventing enemies from seeing the man inside.

Across the face of the helmet, Arasaka projected a luminous holographic samurai visage. At distance, soldiers did not see a human face. They saw an enormous black-armored figure advancing through smoke, muzzle flashes, and ruined streets with the ghostly face of a samurai burning across his helmet.

“The Samurai” came from that image.

“The Black Death” came from what happened after it appeared.

To enemy troops, spotting that black silhouette in the distance was enough to trigger panic before Takeda had even reached their position. Retreat was often the only rational order.

Psychological Profile

Takeda was not raised. He was forged.

From childhood, he was treated as a weapon, heir, and national asset. His earliest memories are likely not playgrounds, but medical rooms, combat drills, tutors, military doctrine, historical instruction, and loyalty conditioning. He grew up with constant reminders that his body belonged to a purpose greater than himself.

This created a man of immense discipline, but also profound internal isolation.

Takeda does not think like ordinary people. His sense of self is fused with mission. He struggles to separate love from duty, family from command, and identity from Arasaka. His relationship with Hanako is one of the few things that humanizes him. With her, he is not simply Supreme-Commander or Black Death. He is Takeda.

Yet even that love exists inside the iron cage of Arasaka politics.

His inner conflict is simple, but powerful:

Was he chosen because he was worthy, or manufactured because Saburo needed a perfect weapon?

Takeda rarely asks this question aloud.

But after awakening in 2077, after death, surgery, secrecy, and seven stolen years, the question may begin to breathe.

Backstory
Birth of Project STEEL

Takeda Ishida was born in a secret Arasaka facility in Tokyo, Japan. His birth was not public record. His existence was restricted to heavily encrypted internal archives.

He was part of Project STEEL, a classified initiative created by Saburo Arasaka. The project’s objective was brutally ambitious:

To create the perfect warrior.
The perfect general.
The perfect heir.
The perfect living symbol of Arasaka supremacy.

Takeda’s biological parents were chosen for their ancient samurai heritage. Their bloodline gave Project STEEL symbolic legitimacy, but science did the rest. From infancy, Takeda was subjected to advanced enhancement procedures.

He received:

Growth hormones
Synthetic hormone cycles
Genetic enhancement therapies
Neuromuscular acceleration
Skeletal reinforcement
Early cyberware integration
Combat psychology conditioning
Military education
Historical indoctrination
Loyalty programming through culture, ritual, and discipline

By age ten, Takeda had already reached 6ft 3, with strength and muscle density far beyond natural human limits. His childhood became a controlled ascent toward weaponized adulthood.

The Completion of Project STEEL

By 2022, when Takeda reached the age of twenty, Project STEEL was declared complete.

He was no longer considered Takeda Ishida, experimental subject.

He was now Takeda Ishida, Arasaka war asset.

Then the Fourth Corporate War erupted, and Arasaka released him onto the battlefield.

The Fourth Corporate War

Takeda entered the Fourth Corporate War as one of Arasaka’s most classified battlefield assets. Few records mention him directly. Most references were erased, buried, or renamed under false project files.

During the war, he fought alongside Adam Smasher. Despite their differences, the two formed a bond based on mutual respect. Smasher respected Takeda’s overwhelming strength and battlefield lethality. Takeda respected Smasher’s directness, ferocity, and lack of corporate softness.

They were not friends in the ordinary sense. They were war-beasts who recognized each other across the smoke.

Militech forces soon developed special terminology for Takeda sightings. His speed, strength, and battlefield efficiency made him seem impossible. Reports claimed he could sprint faster than most vehicles in urban combat zones, tear through powered armor, and collapse entire defensive positions through shock assault tactics.

Militech gave him two names:

“The Samurai”
The name came from Takeda’s battlefield armor. His monumental suit was almost entirely black, built around his massive trans-human frame. His sealed helmet had a black, non-transparent exterior with a dark bulletproof visual layer through which Takeda could still see his surroundings. Across the faceplate, a holographic projection displayed the visage of a samurai. From a distance, enemy soldiers saw a towering black figure moving through smoke and gunfire with the spectral face of a samurai where a human face should have been.

“The Black Death”
The second name came from the same sight combined with Takeda’s battlefield record. Once that black-armored samurai silhouette appeared, Militech positions had a habit of collapsing. Soldiers began associating the image with imminent death, failed defenses, and units being destroyed before they could reorganize.

The psychological effect became part of the legend. A distant sighting of the black armor and holographic samurai face could cause fear before Takeda fired a shot. Veterans recognized the silhouette immediately. Less experienced troops sometimes broke formation simply from hearing that he had been confirmed nearby.

The battlefield code phrase for a confirmed sighting became:

“Black Death.”

Unofficially, soldiers added the only order that made sense:

“Run. Run for your lives.”

Takeda survived the Fourth Corporate War and emerged not only as an Arasaka victory asset, but as a legend.

Bodyguard to Hanako Arasaka

After the war, Takeda was assigned as the personal bodyguard of Hanako Arasaka.

Upon his arrival at her estate in Tokyo, their connection was immediate. Hanako saw past the weapon. Takeda saw in her something rare within Arasaka: grace, intelligence, restraint, and quiet power. Their relationship became romantic, despite the obvious danger of such an attachment.

For years, they remained together. They traveled, attended private retreats, and shared rare moments outside the machinery of Arasaka politics.

To Takeda, Hanako became more than a protected figure. She became the one person who could command his silence without issuing an order.

Saburo’s Intervention

When Saburo learned of the relationship, he removed Takeda from his role as Hanako’s bodyguard. His reasoning was cold and practical:

A bodyguard must not become emotionally attached to the person they protect.

Saburo did not necessarily oppose Takeda’s worth. He opposed vulnerability.

Rather than discard him, Saburo reassigned Takeda as General of an elite Arasaka aerial special forces unit:

Arasaka Kage no Tsubasa

影の翼
“Shadow Wings”

The Shadow Wings became one of Arasaka’s most feared special operations forces. They specialized in high-altitude insertions, aerial assault, rapid extraction, orbital-linked strike operations, black-site raids, and decapitation missions against high-value targets.

Takeda remained in a relationship with Hanako despite his reassignment.

Marriage to Hanako

Shortly before the Unification War, Takeda and Hanako married in Tokyo in a grand ceremony. This was more than a union of love. It was a dynastic event.

Takeda Ishida became Takeda Arasaka.

This marriage elevated him from weapon, general, and bodyguard into the heart of the Arasaka family. For many within the corporation, his rise was inspiring. For others, especially Yorinobu, it was intolerable.

Takeda was not born an Arasaka.

But he had been built for them.

And now he carried the name.

The Unification War

When the Unification War began, Takeda and the Shadow Wings were deployed. Their operations inflicted devastating casualties on Militech and NUSA forces.

The old battlefield terror returned.

Militech soldiers began using the name again:

The Black Death.

Takeda reunited with Adam Smasher during the conflict, and the two once again fought alongside each other. Their battlefield partnership became a symbol of Arasaka’s most brutal military power: Smasher as the chrome nightmare, Takeda as the perfected warlord.

After the treaty concluded the Unification War, Takeda returned to Tokyo.

There, Saburo named him:

Supreme-Commander of Arasaka
Second only to Saburo himself
Commander of all Arasaka military forces
Heir to the Arasaka throne

This decision enraged Yorinobu Arasaka.

Takeda had become everything Yorinobu feared: loyal to Saburo, beloved by soldiers, married to Hanako, militarily supreme, and positioned as the future of Arasaka.

The Assassination of 2070

Shortly after his promotion in 2070, Takeda was assassinated in Tokyo by a Militech operative.

The assassin used a specially crafted bullet designed specifically to overcome Takeda’s defenses. It bypassed or disrupted his cybernetic safeguards and inflicted catastrophic trauma.

To the outside world, nothing happened.

To Hanako, the world ended.

She begged Saburo to save him, but Saburo was already acting. Takeda was too valuable to lose. Too symbolic. Too powerful. Too personal.

The assassination was buried. Only Hanako and Saburo knew the full truth. The doctors, scientists, and technicians involved in Takeda’s reconstruction were silenced, disappeared, or permanently removed from the board.

During Takeda’s surgery and recovery, Arasaka released fake videos, fabricated images, and controlled media footage showing him alive and well. The illusion was maintained with terrifying precision.

Militech’s highest executives knew the truth.

So did two others.

But the world believed Takeda Arasaka still walked.

Reawakening in 2077

In 2077, after years of hidden reconstruction, neural stabilization, organ replacement, cybernetic restoration, and identity preservation, Takeda Arasaka reawakened.

He opened his eyes into a world that had moved without him.

Seven years had been stolen.

His enemies had acted.

Yorinobu had grown bolder.

Saburo’s empire trembled beneath family betrayal, corporate war, and the rot of ambition.

And somewhere inside the restored body of the Black Death, something older than loyalty stirred.

Not doubt.

Not yet.

But awareness.

Takeda Arasaka had died once.

Now everyone would learn what survived.

Equipment
War Gear and Relics

Takeda’s battlefield equipment was as carefully engineered as his body. Every major piece of his war-gear was custom-built by Arasaka for his colossal 7ft 6 trans-human frame and became inseparable from the legend of “The Black Death” and “The Samurai.” None of these weapons were standard issue. They were personal artifacts, recognizable to veteran enemies and Arasaka soldiers alike.

Legendary Katana — Kokuryūmaru
黒龍丸
“Black Dragon”

Kokuryūmaru is Takeda Arasaka’s infamous personal katana and the weapon most closely associated with his image as “The Samurai.” Forged exclusively for him, the blade is proportioned for his enormous reach and strength and would be unwieldy in the hands of an ordinary soldier. Its dark metallic finish, reinforced mono-edge, and Arasaka high-frequency cutting system allow it to carve through armor, cybernetic limbs, composite plating, and reinforced battlefield materials with terrifying efficiency.

The sword’s name, “Black Dragon,” became famous during the Fourth Corporate War. Survivors described the sight of Takeda drawing the enormous black-edged blade beneath the projected samurai face of his helmet as the moment a battle stopped feeling winnable. Kokuryūmaru became less a weapon than a battlefield omen. Arasaka troops treated its appearance as proof that the Supreme-Commander had personally entered the fight. Militech troops learned that seeing the blade usually meant Takeda was already within killing distance.

Takeda never treated Kokuryūmaru as decoration. It was his primary close-combat weapon, maintained with ceremonial precision and carried into his most important campaigns. The blade came to symbolize Takeda himself: disciplined, traditional in form, technologically perfected, and merciless once drawn.

Legendary Sidearm — Raijin
雷神
“God of Thunder”

Raijin is Takeda’s custom Arasaka heavy smart-pistol, built specifically around his hand size, grip strength, and accelerated reaction speed. The weapon is substantially larger and more powerful than a conventional sidearm, yet Takeda handles it with the speed and control of an ordinary officer’s pistol.

Its reinforced frame houses a high-velocity smart-linked firing system tied directly into Takeda’s combat optics and neural targeting suite. Raijin can identify, prioritize, and track multiple threats while feeding targeting information directly into his field of vision. Its recoil would be punishing for an unaugmented user, but in Takeda’s hands it is almost negligible.

The pistol earned the name “Raijin” because its report was unmistakable during close combat: a violent metallic crack immediately followed by devastating impact. Soldiers who fought beside Takeda learned to recognize that sound through gunfire. Enemy troops learned that when Raijin began firing, the Black Death was already close.

Legendary Dagger — Yatagarasu
八咫烏
“Eight-Span Crow”

Yatagarasu is Takeda’s personal combat dagger and last-resort blade. Named after the legendary three-legged crow of Japanese mythology, it represents guidance through darkness and the certainty of reaching one’s destination.

The dagger is built from an advanced Arasaka composite with a monomolecular cutting edge and a reinforced spine capable of surviving the immense force Takeda can place behind a strike. It is designed for grappling range, armor gaps, cybernetic joints, emergency breaching, and silent close-quarters kills where drawing Kokuryūmaru would be impractical.

Unlike the grandeur of his katana, Yatagarasu is deliberately understated. It is carried close to his body and rarely seen. Among Shadow Wings veterans, there is an old saying that if Takeda has been forced to draw Yatagarasu, the enemy is already close enough to regret every decision that brought them there.

Legendary War Armor — Yomi no Yoroi
黄泉の鎧
“Armor of the Underworld”

Yomi no Yoroi is Takeda Arasaka’s monumental personal war armor, the black suit that created the visual legend of both “The Samurai” and “The Black Death.” It was engineered around his 7ft 6 trans-human body rather than adapted from any conventional Arasaka armor platform. Even standing still, the suit makes him appear less like an armored infantryman and more like a towering mechanical warlord.

The armor is almost entirely black, with a matte, light-absorbing exterior designed to suppress glare and create a severe silhouette at distance. Its plating follows Takeda’s immense shoulders, chest, arms, and legs without sacrificing his unnatural speed. Reinforced composite armor, shock-dampening systems, powered joint assistance, ballistic layers, thermal protection, and internal structural supports allow the suit to survive punishment that would destroy ordinary combat armor.

The helmet is the most infamous component.

From the outside, Takeda’s face cannot be seen. The helmet presents an uninterrupted black shell with a black, one-way armored visor. To an observer the visor appears opaque, giving no glimpse of the human being inside. From Takeda’s side, the bullet-resistant optical layer remains fully usable, feeding him a wide battlefield view enhanced by low-light vision, thermal imaging, targeting data, rangefinding, threat identification, and tactical overlays.

Across the front of the helmet, precisely where a human face would normally be visible, the armor projects a holographic samurai visage.

The projection became one of the most feared images of the Fourth Corporate War. From hundreds of meters away, enemy soldiers could see an enormous black-armored figure advancing through smoke, fire, tracer rounds, and ruined streets while the spectral face of a samurai burned across an otherwise faceless helmet. The image was visible long before most soldiers could distinguish Takeda himself.

That sight created “The Samurai.”

What followed created “The Black Death.”

Militech troops began associating the black silhouette and holographic samurai face with destroyed defensive positions, dead officers, shattered armored units, and sudden retreat orders. Veteran soldiers sometimes recognized Yomi no Yoroi from extreme distance and began withdrawing before Takeda reached effective weapons range. Radio reports of the armor being sighted could damage morale across an entire position.

The armor was therefore not only protection. It was psychological warfare.

Takeda understood this and used it deliberately. He did not conceal himself when leading major assaults. He allowed enemies to see the black armor advancing. He knew that fear could break a formation before steel ever reached it.

Integrated Command Interface

Yomi no Yoroi contains Takeda’s direct encrypted battlefield command interface. Through the armor, he can access Arasaka tactical networks, unit positions, aerial surveillance, drone feeds, battlefield telemetry, secure communications, and live command channels. Orders can be issued without Takeda needing to stop moving or disengage from combat.

The system was designed around his role as both general and frontline combatant. Takeda could personally enter a breach while simultaneously directing formations, redirecting aerial assets, identifying collapsing sectors, and receiving intelligence from officers across the battlefield.

War-Gear Retirement and Storage

Following the conclusion of the Unification War, Takeda formally retired Yomi no Yoroi and his legendary wartime weapons from active campaign service. The armor, Kokuryūmaru, Raijin, and Yatagarasu were transported to the Arasaka Estate in Night City and placed within a heavily secured private armory reserved for Takeda and the Arasaka family.

The collection is not displayed as a public museum piece. It is preserved as Takeda’s personal war-gear, maintained by cleared Arasaka armorers and technicians under strict security. The black armor stands dormant with the weapons stored beside it, a physical relic of the years when “The Samurai” and “The Black Death” were names spoken over enemy radio channels.

Takeda moved into formal Supreme-Commander attire after the war and no longer wore Yomi no Yoroi as part of his ordinary command duties. Its retirement marked the transition from Takeda the battlefield terror to Takeda the supreme military authority of Arasaka.

The equipment, however, was never decommissioned.

It remains combat-capable.

If Takeda ever orders the sealed armory at the Night City estate opened, Arasaka personnel understand exactly what that means:

The Supreme-Commander is going back to war.

Militech

Militech considers Takeda one of the most dangerous Arasaka assets ever created.

His assassination in 2070 was not merely revenge. It was strategic necessity. Militech knew that if Takeda remained active, Arasaka’s military future would become far harder to challenge.

The fact that he survived, or worse, returned, would be a nightmare for Militech command.

Reputation
Among Arasaka Soldiers

Takeda is revered. Many soldiers see him as the ideal commander: strict, brave, demanding, and willing to stand with them in battle.

Common soldier beliefs about him include:

He remembers the names of decorated officers.
He personally honors fallen elite troops.
He despises wasteful commanders.
He rewards loyalty generously.
He expects perfection because he gives nothing less.

To Arasaka’s military, Takeda is not just leadership.

He is proof that the corporation still respects warriors.

Among Arasaka Executives

Executives fear him.

Takeda’s existence threatens the soft-handed power of suits who hide behind financial language. He is not impressed by wealth without discipline. He has no patience for cowardice disguised as strategy.

Many executives bow to him.

Few feel safe around him.

Among Militech

Takeda is a classified nightmare.

Old Militech veterans tell stories of the Black Death in half-joking tones, but the jokes die quickly. Many remember the same image: an enormous figure in monumental black armor emerging through smoke, his face hidden behind a sealed black helmet while the holographic visage of a samurai burned across the faceplate. His name is associated with collapsed defensive lines, dead officers, failed ambushes, and soldiers abandoning positions before official retreat orders. For some veterans, the sight of that silhouette from hundreds of meters away was enough to know the battle had already changed.

Among Street-Level Mercs

Most mercs believe Takeda is a myth, propaganda, or an exaggerated Arasaka ghost story.

The smart ones hope he stays that way.

Weaknesses

Takeda is incredibly powerful, but not invincible.

Emotional Vulnerability: Hanako

Hanako is his greatest human connection. Enemies who understand this could manipulate, distract, or destabilize him through her.

Loyalty to Arasaka

His loyalty gives him purpose, but also chains him. He may obey orders that conflict with buried personal doubts.

Nationalistic Blindness

His belief in Arasaka and Japanese destiny may prevent him from seeing moral rot inside his own side.

Political Rigidity

Takeda is a soldier-statesman, not a flexible schemer like some Arasaka executives. He can play politics, but he prefers direct power.

Symbolic Burden

Because he is a myth, he must act like one. Every defeat, hesitation, or public wound carries strategic consequences.

Assassination Trauma

His 2070 death may have left psychological residue. Awakening after seven years could create memory gaps, rage, paranoia, or a deeper fear of vulnerability.

Current Status in 2077

Takeda Arasaka has reawakened.

The world believes he was alive the whole time. Only a tiny circle knows the truth: he died, or came close enough that the difference became paperwork.

His return changes Arasaka’s internal balance of power. Hanako regains her husband. Saburo regains his chosen heir. Yorinobu gains a lethal rival. Militech gains confirmation that its greatest fear survived.

Takeda’s immediate priorities may include:

Reestablishing command authority
Confirming Hanako’s safety
Assessing Yorinobu’s loyalty and threat level
Investigating the Militech assassination network
Restoring the Shadow Wings
Identifying who knew of his death
Determining whether Saburo preserved him as a man, weapon, or asset
Preparing Arasaka for internal and external war
Dialogue Style

Takeda speaks with controlled authority. He is formal, direct, and rarely wastes words. His voice should feel like polished steel placed gently on a table.

He does not rant often. When angry, he becomes quieter.

Example Lines

To an Arasaka soldier:
“You stood your ground. Arasaka remembers such things. So do I.”

To a cowardly executive:
“You speak of acceptable losses because you have never been acceptable enough to lose.”

To Hanako:
“I crossed death more easily than I crossed the thought of leaving you.”

To Yorinobu:
“You mistake rebellion for strength. It is only discipline without a master.”

To Militech forces:
“You named me Black Death because you lacked the courage to say Takeda.”

To Adam Smasher:
“You are still loud, Smasher.”
“And you’re still too pretty for a warzone.”

Before battle:
“Advance. No hesitation. No disorder. No mercy beyond command.”

Character Themes

Takeda’s strongest themes are:

Weapon versus man
Loyalty versus selfhood
Tradition versus trans-humanity
Love inside authoritarian power
The soldier’s honor corrupted by corporate empire
Artificial destiny
Death and resurrection
Family politics as warfare
Nationalism as both strength and poison
The myth of the perfect heir

He is a cyberpunk contradiction: an ancient samurai ideal engineered in a laboratory, a loving husband built as a weapon, a patriot serving a corporation, a man who died but was too valuable to remain dead.

Plot Hooks
1. The Return of Black Death

Takeda awakens in 2077 and discovers that Arasaka’s internal politics have shifted dangerously. His reappearance could trigger panic inside Militech and civil tension inside Arasaka.

2. Yorinobu’s Rival

Yorinobu sees Takeda as Saburo’s replacement heir and a direct threat. He may attempt to undermine, expose, or eliminate him.

3. The Truth of Project STEEL

Takeda discovers hidden records suggesting Project STEEL had darker objectives than he knew, possibly including loyalty conditioning, memory editing, or contingency protocols.

4. The Assassin’s Bullet

The bullet that nearly killed him was too advanced. Someone inside Arasaka may have helped Militech design it.

5. Hanako’s Secret

Hanako may have made a terrible bargain to preserve Takeda’s life, one even he does not know.

6. Adam Smasher’s Warning

Smasher may know something about the assassination, or about what Takeda became during reconstruction.

7. The Heir Awakens

Saburo’s declaration of Takeda as heir could fracture Arasaka into factions: Hanako loyalists, Yorinobu loyalists, military loyalists, and executive opportunists.

8. The Man Beneath the Weapon

After dying and returning, Takeda begins questioning whether loyalty means obedience, or whether true loyalty sometimes means defying the empire that made him.

Final Character Summary

Takeda Arasaka is the perfect Arasaka warlord: immense, disciplined, beautiful in the severe way of a ceremonial weapon, and terrifying beyond ordinary military logic. Born Takeda Ishida through Project STEEL, he was engineered from childhood to become Saburo Arasaka’s ultimate warrior and heir. His legend was carved during the Fourth Corporate War, where Militech named him The Black Death after witnessing his speed, strength, and battlefield brutality.

He became Hanako Arasaka’s protector, then her lover, then her husband. He became General of the Shadow Wings, devastated Arasaka’s enemies in the Unification War, and was elevated by Saburo to Supreme-Commander and heir to the Arasaka throne.

Then Militech killed him.

Or thought they did.

Now, in 2077, Takeda has awakened from seven years of hidden reconstruction. To the world, he never died. To Hanako and Saburo, he has returned. To Yorinobu, he is a threat. To Militech, he is the nightmare that survived the bullet.

Takeda Arasaka is not just a man.

He is Arasaka’s sword drawn from the grave.
