# LORE-BOOK: NIGHT CITY

## THE CITY OF DREAMS

Night City is an independent metropolis on the coast of Northern California and the principal setting of Cyberpunk 2077.

By 2077, it is one of the most influential, technologically advanced, wealthy, violent, corrupt, and culturally significant cities in North America.

Night City sells itself as a place where anyone can become anything.

A nobody can become a celebrity.

A street kid can become a millionaire.

A mercenary can become a legend.

A corporation can build an empire.

Someone with enough talent, desperation, money, violence, or luck can rise astonishingly quickly.

They can fall just as quickly.

Night City is called the City of Dreams because dreams are one of its most profitable industries.

People arrive constantly believing they will be the exception.

The city has built an entire culture around that belief.

# POLITICAL STATUS

Night City is not an ordinary American city.

By 2077, it functions as an independent city-state.

It is geographically located on territory historically associated with California, but it is politically separate from the New United States of America.

Night City maintains its own municipal government, laws, police force, economic arrangements, corporate relationships, and political interests.

Its independence is extremely important to its identity.

The NUSA would prefer Night City to fall under federal authority.

Many corporations and Night City power brokers strongly oppose that possibility.

This makes Night City politically valuable.

It sits between competing corporate, regional, and national interests while functioning as one of the largest economic centers on the western coast of North America.

# WHO ACTUALLY RULES NIGHT CITY?

Officially, Night City is governed by elected municipal institutions.

There is a Mayor.

There is a City Council.

There are courts.

There are municipal departments.

There is a police force.

There are laws and regulations.

That is the official version.

In reality, political power is distributed between numerous competing factions.

The city government possesses genuine authority, but major corporations exert enormous influence over public policy.

Megacorporations finance projects, provide security, employ enormous portions of the population, control essential infrastructure, lobby politicians, influence elections, and possess enough military force that the government cannot simply order them around.

Major gangs control portions of the streets.

Fixers control sections of the underground economy.

Wealthy families, investors, criminal organizations, media companies, security contractors, and neighborhood communities all possess smaller spheres of influence.

Night City therefore does not have one ruler.

It has an ecosystem of power.

# THE CORPORATE PRESENCE

Corporations are physically woven into Night City.

Their skyscrapers dominate the skyline.

Their advertisements cover the streets.

Their employees fill apartment towers.

Their security forces protect facilities.

Their products fill stores.

Their weapons circulate among gangs and mercenaries.

Their medical technologies keep people alive.

Their factories employ thousands.

Their political representatives influence City Hall.

The largest corporations possess buildings and compounds that function almost like sovereign territory.

Walking into a major corporate facility can mean entering an environment where corporate security matters more than municipal police.

Among the most powerful corporations operating in Night City are organizations such as:

Arasaka.

Militech.

Biotechnica.

Petrochem.

Kang Tao.

Night Corp.

Trauma Team International.

Kiroshi.

Zetatech.

Orbital Air.

And numerous others.

Corporate influence differs between districts.

City Center is the most obvious concentration of corporate power, but corporate property exists throughout the metropolis.

# RICHARD NIGHT

Night City began as the dream of Richard Night.

Night was a wealthy and ambitious businessman who became increasingly concerned about the collapse, violence, corruption, and instability spreading throughout American cities during the late twentieth century.

His solution was extraordinarily ambitious.

Rather than repair an existing city, he intended to build a new one.

A privately planned metropolis.

Clean.

Efficient.

Technologically advanced.

Business-friendly.

Secure.

Modern.

Multicultural.

And largely protected from the political chaos affecting the rest of the country.

The project attracted enormous corporate investment.

Construction began around the region of Morro Bay.

The city was originally called Coronado City.

Locals increasingly referred to it as Night's City because Richard Night was so closely associated with the project.

# THE DEATH OF THE DREAM

Richard Night never saw his vision completed.

In 1998, he was murdered.

His killer was never conclusively identified.

Following his death, Coronado City was officially renamed Night City in his honor.

The symbolism was almost cruel.

A city designed to escape corruption became consumed by it.

Organized crime expanded rapidly.

Corporations competed for control.

Gangs grew.

Political corruption increased.

Neighborhoods became battlegrounds.

Night's carefully planned urban experiment fractured into competing territories.

The utopia had barely opened its doors before people began carving pieces out of it.

# THE MOB YEARS

During Night City's early decades, organized crime became enormously powerful.

Criminal syndicates gained control over businesses, unions, construction projects, prostitution, drugs, gambling, protection rackets, and other parts of the urban economy.

Violence became widespread.

The police were increasingly unable to maintain control.

Eventually, the corporations decided organized crime was interfering too heavily with business.

This resulted in the Mob War.

Corporate forces, private soldiers, mercenaries, and heavily armed security personnel attacked organized crime operations across the city.

The corporations won.

They did not eliminate crime.

They simply demonstrated who possessed the greater capacity for violence.

From then onward, organized criminals generally understood that interfering directly with the largest corporations could bring consequences far beyond ordinary gang warfare.

# CORPORATE NIGHT CITY

After corporate forces crushed much of the old mob establishment, corporate influence over Night City increased dramatically.

Corporate security helped police portions of the city.

Corporate money rebuilt districts.

Corporate-backed politicians gained influence.

The safest and wealthiest areas became increasingly designed around corporate interests.

Night City began developing the political structure recognizable in later decades.

Official government continued to exist.

Corporate power increasingly existed alongside it.

# THE FOURTH CORPORATE WAR

Night City suffered one of the greatest catastrophes in its history during the Fourth Corporate War.

The conflict eventually centered heavily around Arasaka and Militech.

It spread across the world through military operations, economic warfare, corporate espionage, naval battles, cyberwarfare, sabotage, and proxy conflicts.

Night City became a major battleground.

# THE 2023 NUCLEAR DISASTER

In 2023, a nuclear weapon detonated at Arasaka's Night City headquarters.

The explosion devastated the city center.

Buildings collapsed.

Infrastructure failed.

Radiation contaminated large areas.

Hundreds of thousands were killed either immediately or through the wider consequences of the disaster.

Government services were overwhelmed.

Huge numbers of survivors were displaced.

Entire neighborhoods became ruins.

The center of one of the most ambitious cities on Earth had been transformed into a radioactive disaster zone.

# THE TIME OF THE RED

The decades following the Fourth Corporate War became known as the Time of the Red.

Night City spent years recovering.

Refugee populations crowded into surviving neighborhoods.

Ruined buildings were demolished.

Debris was removed or repurposed.

Infrastructure had to be rebuilt.

Trade networks were damaged.

Corporate influence shifted.

Some sections of the old city disappeared completely.

Others were reconstructed under entirely new names and purposes.

For years, the city that had once promised technological utopia resembled an enormous construction site surrounding a wound.

# RECONSTRUCTION

Night City rebuilt aggressively.

By the 2050s and 2060s, enormous investment had returned.

New skyscrapers appeared.

Megabuildings housed huge populations.

Corporate districts were reconstructed.

Transportation networks expanded.

Entertainment districts flourished.

Foreign investment returned.

Night City transformed itself once again into a global symbol of wealth and technological modernity.

The rebuilt city deliberately hides much of its history beneath new construction.

A luxury tower may stand where a battlefield once existed.

A shopping district may occupy ground created from demolished ruins.

The city rarely preserves tragedy unless tragedy can be commercialized.

# THE UNIFICATION WAR

During the 2060s, the New United States attempted to bring independent territories back under federal control.

This eventually became the Unification War.

Night City faced the possibility of being absorbed into the NUSA.

NUSA and Militech forces approached the city.

Night City's government sought outside protection.

Arasaka returned to Night City in force.

The threat of Arasaka intervention prevented a direct assault and helped preserve the city's independence.

A settlement followed.

Night City remained autonomous.

Arasaka's political and military presence within the city became enormous once again.

# NIGHT CITY IN 2077

By 2077, Night City has largely recovered physically from its earlier disasters.

The scars remain socially and politically.

The city is spectacular from a distance.

Enormous skyscrapers dominate the skyline.

Holographic advertisements glow against the night.

Elevated highways cut between districts.

Luxury AVs travel above streets packed with ordinary cars.

Megabuildings contain thousands of residents.

Nightclubs operate throughout the night.

Street markets fill narrow alleys.

Corporate plazas gleam beneath enormous advertisements.

From above, Night City looks like civilization's triumph.

At street level, the picture changes.

Homeless camps occupy forgotten corners.

Gang members watch intersections.

Police holograms mark murder scenes.

Trash piles accumulate beside expensive buildings.

Scavengers hunt for victims.

Street vendors sell synthetic food.

Mercenaries negotiate killings in bars.

Children play beneath corporate advertising screens.

Night City contains immense wealth and immense poverty within walking distance of each other.

# GEOGRAPHY

Night City is built around Del Coronado Bay on the Pacific coast.

Its geography has been heavily altered through decades of construction.

Land reclamation, artificial development, highways, industrial zones, ports, and megaprojects have reshaped much of the original coastline.

The city contains dense urban districts surrounded by industrial territory and eventually the Badlands.

Water defines much of the western side of Night City.

The Badlands dominate its outskirts.

Moving only a relatively short distance can therefore transform the environment from dense vertical metropolis into open desert and industrial wasteland.

# THE SIX MAJOR DISTRICTS

Night City is divided into six principal districts:

City Center.

Watson.

Westbrook.

Heywood.

Santo Domingo.

Pacifica.

Each district possesses multiple sub-districts.

District boundaries matter culturally.

Someone saying they are from Watson or Heywood communicates information about class, community, lifestyle, and reputation.

Night City residents frequently identify strongly with the places where they grew up.

# CITY CENTER

Sub-districts:

Corporate Plaza.

Downtown.

City Center is the financial and corporate heart of Night City.

It is the skyline seen in advertisements.

This is where corporations demonstrate their power.

Immense towers belonging to Arasaka, Militech, Petrochem, Biotechnica, Kang Tao, and other corporations dominate the area.

Security is heavy.

Architecture is monumental.

Money moves constantly.

Corporate employees rush between offices.

Luxury vehicles fill the streets.

Executives arrive in aerial vehicles.

High-end restaurants, clubs, shops, hotels, and apartments cater to people with substantial disposable income.

City Center is cleaner and more heavily protected than most districts.

That does not make it safe.

It makes violence more controlled.

Street gangs are less openly powerful here because corporations and police possess stronger security.

Corporate espionage, assassination, sabotage, financial crime, covert operations, and mercenary work remain common.

In City Center, murder is more likely to wear an expensive suit.

## CORPORATE PLAZA

Corporate Plaza represents Night City's concentration of megacorporate power.

Corporate towers surround heavily monitored public spaces.

Security systems are everywhere.

Corporate employees move through the district in enormous numbers.

The atmosphere is clean, imposing, expensive, and deliberately intimidating.

Someone openly causing trouble here can attract extremely serious attention.

Corporate Plaza communicates one message:

The corporations are powerful enough to build monuments to themselves.

## DOWNTOWN

Downtown is more commercial and socially active than Corporate Plaza.

It contains offices, luxury hotels, restaurants, clubs, apartments, cultural venues, shops, and entertainment.

The wealthy mix with corporate workers, tourists, celebrities, mercenaries, criminals, and people hoping to appear wealthier than they actually are.

The main streets may be glamorous.

The alleys behind them can be considerably less respectable.

# WATSON

Sub-districts:

Little China.

Kabuki.

Northside.

Arasaka Waterfront.

Watson once appeared destined to become one of Night City's most prosperous areas.

Investment poured into the district.

Corporations built offices.

Entertainment venues opened.

Medical facilities operated there.

Then economic disaster and corporate withdrawal crushed much of Watson's prosperity.

By 2077, Watson is densely populated, heavily commercialized, frequently dangerous, and strongly influenced by gangs.

Cheap apartments, street markets, warehouses, industrial areas, clinics, clubs, workshops, restaurants, and criminal operations all overlap.

Watson embodies urban survival.

People improvise.

Businesses operate wherever space exists.

Legal and illegal economies blend together.

## LITTLE CHINA

Little China is an extremely dense urban neighborhood.

Apartment blocks, small businesses, shops, food stands, alleyways, traffic, and crowds define much of the area.

It is heavily populated and comparatively central.

People from wildly different backgrounds pass through constantly.

It is the kind of neighborhood where almost anything can be purchased if someone knows which door to knock on.

## KABUKI

Kabuki contains one of Night City's most famous markets.

Vendors sell electronics, food, weapons, cyberware components, clothing, pharmaceuticals, counterfeit products, stolen goods, and countless other items.

The difference between legitimate commerce and black-market activity can become extremely thin.

Kabuki rewards people who know how to bargain.

It punishes people who look lost.

## NORTHSIDE

Northside is heavily industrial.

Factories, warehouses, construction sites, abandoned buildings, garages, and shipping infrastructure dominate large sections of the area.

Maelstrom maintains a strong presence here.

The district attracts people who need industrial space, cheap property, illegal workshops, warehouses, or somewhere to disappear.

## ARASAKA WATERFRONT

The Arasaka Waterfront contains heavily secured industrial and port infrastructure belonging primarily to Arasaka.

Unlike much of Watson, corporate control here is extremely strong.

Trespassing is significantly more dangerous when the people guarding the fence possess corporate military hardware.

# WESTBROOK

Sub-districts:

Japantown.

Charter Hill.

North Oak.

Westbrook is one of Night City's wealthiest and most desirable districts.

It is where successful people go to demonstrate that they are successful.

Luxury apartments.

Nightclubs.

Restaurants.

Hotels.

Shopping.

Entertainment.

Corporate housing.

Expensive vehicles.

Designer cyberware.

Westbrook sells lifestyle.

People visit to party, spend money, network, show off, make deals, and pretend tomorrow does not exist.

## JAPANTOWN

Japantown is one of Night City's largest entertainment centers.

The district contains hotels, clubs, bars, restaurants, gambling, braindance establishments, shops, and extensive nightlife.

Japanese cultural influence is highly visible, though much of it has been commercialized and transformed by decades of Night City culture.

The Tyger Claws maintain substantial influence throughout the area.

Many apparently legitimate businesses exist within networks of gang ownership, protection, or cooperation.

Japantown rarely sleeps.

## CHARTER HILL

Charter Hill represents aspirational wealth.

It is not necessarily reserved for the ultra-rich.

Successful professionals, corporate employees, and upper-middle-class residents may live here.

Apartments are expensive.

Security is better than in most districts.

The streets are cleaner.

Residents often possess good jobs and substantial debt.

Charter Hill represents people who have successfully climbed several steps up Night City's ladder and are terrified of falling back down.

## NORTH OAK

North Oak is where genuine wealth lives.

Large homes.

Private property.

Celebrity residences.

Executives.

Elite security.

Privacy.

Space itself becomes a luxury.

While most Night City residents live surrounded by crowds, North Oak residents can purchase distance from everyone else.

That may be the greatest status symbol in the entire city.

# HEYWOOD

Sub-districts:

The Glen.

Vista del Rey.

Wellsprings.

Heywood is one of Night City's largest residential districts.

More people live here than in many other sections of the metropolis.

It is frequently described as the city's enormous bedroom because so many residents return here after working elsewhere.

Heywood contains sharp class differences.

Some northern and coastal neighborhoods are comfortable, clean, and reasonably prosperous.

Other areas suffer from poverty, gang activity, and declining infrastructure.

Community identity is extremely strong.

Many people raised in Heywood are fiercely loyal to their neighborhood.

The Valentinos are deeply rooted in portions of the district.

6th Street also operates in parts of the wider area.

## THE GLEN

The Glen contains important municipal institutions.

City Hall and other government facilities are located here.

It also contains apartments, businesses, parks, restaurants, and working neighborhoods.

The Glen mixes political authority with ordinary city life.

Fixers, politicians, police, lawyers, criminals, workers, and families can all operate within the same few blocks.

## VISTA DEL REY

Vista del Rey is significantly poorer.

Buildings are less maintained.

Gang influence is stronger.

Crime is more visible.

Residents frequently rely on local relationships and community networks when official institutions fail them.

The Valentinos possess significant cultural and criminal influence here.

## WELLSPRINGS

Wellsprings is generally more prosperous and attractive.

It contains residential areas, commercial property, coastline, restaurants, and comparatively comfortable neighborhoods.

For many Night City residents, getting an apartment in a good part of Heywood represents genuine upward mobility.

# SANTO DOMINGO

Sub-districts:

Arroyo.

Rancho Coronado.

Santo Domingo is one of Night City's major industrial districts.

Power plants.

Factories.

Warehouses.

Construction sites.

Testing grounds.

Industrial facilities.

Megabuildings.

Much of the physical infrastructure that keeps Night City functioning exists here.

Corporations routinely demolish facilities, construct new ones, test industrial technologies, and reshape the area according to economic needs.

The people who live there often receive fewer benefits than the corporations using their neighborhoods.

## ARROYO

Arroyo is heavily industrial.

Factories, industrial yards, warehouses, power infrastructure, garages, and corporate installations dominate the landscape.

Workers and gangs coexist around enormous industrial complexes.

Pollution and noise are ordinary facts of life.

## RANCHO CORONADO

Rancho Coronado was built as a large planned residential community.

Its suburban design sharply contrasts with Night City's dense urban center.

Rows of homes, shopping areas, artificial landscaping, and broad roads create something resembling an exaggerated suburban dream.

The dream has aged poorly.

Industrial development, economic hardship, gang presence, and deteriorating infrastructure have transformed much of the district.

6th Street possesses substantial influence here.

# PACIFICA

Primary areas include:

Coastview.

West Wind Estate.

Pacifica was supposed to become paradise.

Investors envisioned an enormous luxury resort district filled with hotels, shopping complexes, attractions, beaches, and entertainment.

Construction began.

Then investment collapsed.

Corporations withdrew.

Projects were abandoned.

Half-completed hotels and enormous unfinished structures remained standing.

Residents moved into the ruins.

City services deteriorated.

Gangs expanded.

By 2077, Pacifica is one of the clearest examples of Night City's failed promises.

A district designed for wealthy tourists became an urban combat zone.

## COASTVIEW

Coastview is dominated by unfinished tourist infrastructure and decaying commercial developments.

The Voodoo Boys possess major influence in portions of Pacifica.

The gang maintains deep connections with the local Haitian community and is especially famous for its netrunning expertise.

## WEST WIND ESTATE

West Wind Estate contains further abandoned resort and residential development.

Large structures originally intended for wealthy visitors now exist in dramatically different circumstances.

Pacifica residents have adapted abandoned infrastructure for their own purposes.

# DOGTOWN

Dogtown occupies a sealed section of Pacifica.

It is not an ordinary Night City neighborhood.

The area is controlled by BARGHEST, a heavily armed paramilitary organization.

Dogtown operates like an armed enclave within Night City.

Checkpoints control access.

Armed personnel patrol openly.

Black markets flourish.

Weapons trading is widespread.

Criminals, smugglers, mercenaries, spies, fugitives, politicians, desperate civilians, and foreign operatives all pass through its territory.

Night City's normal institutions possess limited authority there.

Dogtown demonstrates what happens when a section of the city becomes sufficiently fortified that City Hall effectively stops pretending it controls it.

# THE BADLANDS

The Badlands surround Night City but are not considered a normal city district.

The landscape contains desert, abandoned highways, industrial waste, landfills, old infrastructure, mines, power installations, farms, oil fields, corporate facilities, and Nomad territory.

The difference between Night City and the Badlands is immediate.

Inside the city, people are surrounded by buildings and advertisements.

Outside it, the horizon suddenly opens.

Nomad clans use the Badlands extensively.

Smuggling routes cross the region.

Corporate convoys travel through it.

Illegal deals occur far from police attention.

Bodies disappear easily.

The city skyline remains visible from enormous distances, glowing on the horizon like an electronic mirage.

# MEGABUILDINGS

Night City's housing crisis contributed to the construction of enormous residential megabuildings.

A megabuilding is essentially a neighborhood stacked vertically.

Thousands of people may live inside one structure.

Apartments.

Stores.

Food vendors.

Clinics.

Gyms.

Security offices.

Mechanical spaces.

Public areas.

Small businesses.

Criminal operations.

All of these can exist inside the same building.

Some residents can spend days without needing to leave.

Megabuildings vary enormously in condition.

Some remain reasonably functional.

Others suffer from failing infrastructure, gang activity, maintenance problems, abandoned levels, and overcrowding.

They are one of the defining features of Night City's residential landscape.

# HOUSING

Where someone lives says a great deal about their social class.

The poorest residents may sleep:

On streets.

In abandoned buildings.

Inside vehicles.

In temporary shelters.

In improvised camps.

In illegally occupied industrial property.

Working-class residents may live in small apartments or crowded megabuildings.

Successful mercenaries and professionals may rent larger private apartments.

Corporate employees may receive housing connected to employment.

Executives may live in guarded luxury towers.

The extremely wealthy can own villas in areas such as North Oak.

Night City contains almost every imaginable standard of living.

They frequently exist within sight of one another.

# THE STREETS

Night City's streets are rarely quiet.

Traffic.

Advertisements.

Music.

Emergency sirens.

Police broadcasts.

Street vendors.

Construction.

Arguments.

Engines.

Gunfire.

Public announcements.

AVs overhead.

People shouting.

The city produces continuous sensory overload.

Advertising screens compete for attention.

Food stalls produce conflicting smells.

Street lights and holograms saturate major commercial districts.

Industrial neighborhoods smell of metal, chemicals, oil, dust, and exhaust.

Poor neighborhoods accumulate trash faster than municipal services remove it.

Night City should feel lived-in rather than pristine.

# NIGHT AND DAY

Night City does not become inactive during daylight.

Businesses operate.

Workers commute.

Factories run.

Police patrol.

Street markets open.

Corporate offices fill.

Night changes the personality rather than activating the city.

Advertisements become more visually dominant.

Clubs fill.

Illegal businesses become more active.

Street racing increases.

Mercenary meetings occur in bars.

Gangs become more visible in certain neighborhoods.

Red-light districts become crowded.

The neon version of Night City seen in popular imagery is primarily its nocturnal face.

# TRANSPORTATION

Night City contains extensive transportation infrastructure.

Cars remain extremely important.

Major highways connect districts.

Motorcycles are common.

Buses operate throughout the city.

Taxi services exist.

Autonomous vehicles operate alongside manually driven vehicles.

Wealthy individuals may travel by AV, an aerial vehicle capable of bypassing street traffic.

Night City also maintains a rapid transit network known as NCART.

Train and metro infrastructure connects many parts of the metropolis.

Transportation reflects class.

A worker may spend their morning packed into public transit.

A mercenary may ride a motorcycle.

A wealthy executive may cross the same distance without ever touching the ground.

# TRAFFIC

Night City traffic can be brutal.

Millions of residents, freight vehicles, corporate transport, taxis, emergency vehicles, delivery services, motorcycles, and ordinary commuters compete for road space.

Highways dominate enormous portions of the city.

Interchanges twist through residential districts.

Roads can separate neighborhoods as effectively as walls.

Knowing alternative routes can be valuable for mercenaries, criminals, emergency workers, and anyone trying to avoid gang territory.

# POLICE

The Night City Police Department is the city's principal law-enforcement agency.

The NCPD operates precincts, patrol officers, detectives, tactical units, and specialized divisions.

Its effectiveness varies enormously.

Police presence is much stronger in wealthy districts.

Corporate areas may receive immediate responses.

Poor neighborhoods may wait considerably longer.

Some locations receive almost no meaningful protection.

Corruption exists.

Apathy exists.

Good officers exist.

Bad officers exist.

Overworked officers exist.

Officers willing to sell information exist.

The NCPD is not a single unified personality.

It is an enormous institution operating inside an extremely dysfunctional city.

# NCPD RESPONSE

How law enforcement responds depends heavily on:

Where the crime occurred.

Who was attacked.

How wealthy the victim is.

Whether a corporation is involved.

How heavily armed the suspects are.

Whether media attention is present.

Which district is involved.

A corpse in Corporate Plaza can become a major investigation.

A corpse discovered in a forgotten Pacifica building may become another statistic.

# MAX-TAC

MAX-TAC handles extreme threats.

The unit specializes in heavily cyberized and exceptionally dangerous offenders, particularly cyberpsychosis incidents.

MAX-TAC personnel are heavily armed, heavily augmented, and exceptionally aggressive.

Their deployment means an ordinary police response has already become insufficient.

Street wisdom is simple:

If MAX-TAC arrives, do not become interesting.

# CORPORATE SECURITY

Corporate facilities frequently rely on private security rather than ordinary police.

Major corporate security forces may possess:

Military weapons.

Combat cyberware.

Armored vehicles.

Drones.

Netrunners.

Automated defenses.

Surveillance systems.

Elite tactical teams.

Corporate intelligence support.

Security quality depends on the corporation and importance of the facility.

Breaking into a neighborhood warehouse and breaking into an Arasaka research facility are entirely different professions.

# GANG TERRITORY

Gang influence shapes much of Night City.

Graffiti.

Businesses.

Patrols.

A gang may dominate criminal activity while ordinary businesses and residents continue living normal lives.

Some gangs maintain relationships with communities.

Others primarily exploit them.

A neighborhood can therefore contain gang members, civilians, shopkeepers, families, police, mercenaries, and corporate employees simultaneously.

# FOOD

Night City food reflects environmental decline and economic inequality.

Natural meat and high-quality fresh ingredients are expensive.

Many ordinary residents eat heavily processed or synthetic food.

Street vendors are everywhere.

Noodles.

Burritos.

Kibble.

Synthetic meat.

Convenience food.

Vending-machine meals.

Fast food.

Energy drinks.

# WATER

Clean water is infrastructure and therefore money.

Tap water quality depends on location and maintenance.

Bottled and processed beverages are widespread.

Industrial contamination and environmental damage make access to genuinely pristine natural resources far more complicated than it once was.

# ENVIRONMENT

Night City is heavily polluted.

Vehicle emissions.

Industrial waste.

Chemical contamination.

Trash.

Construction dust.

Oil.

Industrial runoff.

Decades of environmental degradation have transformed the surrounding ecosystem.

Certain kinds of severe weather and polluted precipitation can occur.

The city nevertheless maintains parks, artificial landscaping, decorative trees, and controlled green areas.

Natural beauty has increasingly become another luxury product.

# ANIMALS

Wildlife is extremely uncommon inside Night City.

Disease outbreaks and government extermination programs dramatically reduced animal populations.

Many residents can go long periods without seeing ordinary wild animals.

Pets exist but can be expensive and tightly controlled.

This means encountering natural wildlife inside the city can feel far stranger to a Night City resident than seeing someone with chrome arms.

# MUSIC

Metal.

Hip-hop.

Electronic music.

Industrial styles.

Experimental music.

Synth-heavy genres.

Countless cultural hybrids.

Music remains one of the most visible ways people construct identity.

Bands can become political symbols.

Musicians can become celebrities.

Songs can become protest.

Then corporations can sell the protest on a T-shirt.

# FASHION

Night City takes appearance seriously.

Clothing communicates status.

Corporate clothing communicates professionalism and power.

Street fashion communicates neighborhood, gang, subculture, personality, and wealth.

High-fashion clothing can incorporate advanced materials and technology.

People deliberately construct identities through clothing, cyberware, hairstyles, tattoos, implants, and body modification.

Looking dangerous can be useful.

Looking wealthy can be useful.

Looking unimportant can sometimes be even more useful.

# CYBERWARE IN PUBLIC

Cyberware is normal.

A person with artificial eyes does not automatically attract attention.

Neither does someone with a cybernetic arm.

Visible interface ports, reinforced limbs, synthetic skin, optical modifications, and various implants are common.

More extreme combat cyberware attracts additional attention depending on location.

Walking into a restaurant with obvious military-grade modifications is different from possessing ordinary optics.

Context matters.

Night City residents are accustomed to chrome.

They are not necessarily relaxed around walking tanks.

# WEAPONS IN PUBLIC

Weapons are common enough that seeing someone armed is not automatically extraordinary.

Mercenaries.

Gang members.

Security officers.

Police.

Corporate personnel.

Civilians.

Many carry firearms.

Location still matters.

Walking around Watson carrying a pistol may attract little attention.

Entering a heavily secured corporate building carrying an assault rifle will produce a very different response.

Night City is armed.

It is not completely without rules.

# THE INTERNET AND LOCAL NETWORKS

The old global NET was destroyed as a reliably safe public environment decades ago.

Night City's modern digital infrastructure relies heavily on controlled local networks.

Homes, businesses, buildings, corporations, security systems, and municipal infrastructure all connect digitally in various ways.

Netrunners exploit these systems.

Cameras.

Turrets.

Doors.

Databases.

Vehicles.

Personal devices.

Security networks.

Modern crime therefore has both physical and digital dimensions.

# PUBLIC SERVICES

Night City possesses public infrastructure.

Police.

Fire services.

Transportation.

Courts.

Road maintenance.

Emergency systems.

Sanitation.

Utilities.

Municipal government.

The problem is consistency.

Services work much better in some neighborhoods than others.

Wealthy and politically important districts receive greater investment.

Poor neighborhoods are frequently expected to improvise.

A broken streetlight in Corporate Plaza may be repaired rapidly.

A broken elevator in a decaying megabuilding may remain broken for months.

# MEDICAL CARE

Night City contains some of the world's most advanced medicine.

It also contains people who cannot afford basic treatment.

Corporate hospitals and premium clinics can perform extraordinary procedures.

Ripperdocs install sophisticated cyberware.

Trauma Team can conduct armed emergency extraction.

Street clinics treat people who cannot access expensive healthcare.

Unlicensed doctors operate illegally.

Quality ranges from miraculous to horrifying.

The deciding factor is usually money.

# TRAUMA TEAM

Trauma Team represents premium emergency medicine.

Subscribers carry coverage linked to emergency response.

When sufficiently valuable subscribers are critically injured, Trauma Team may deploy an armed medical squad to extract them.

They are not police.

They are not arriving to protect everyone.

Their objective is the subscriber.

Anyone preventing that extraction can become an obstacle.

This produces a strange Night City reality where a gunfight can be occurring in an alley and a military-grade medical team may suddenly arrive because one specific person has expensive insurance.

# FIRE AND EMERGENCY SERVICES

Fire and rescue services still operate but face the same inequality as other municipal institutions.

Dense buildings, aging infrastructure, industrial accidents, gang warfare, cyberware incidents, explosions, and corporate secrecy make emergency response difficult.

Some corporate facilities possess their own emergency systems.

Other neighborhoods may be forced to rely heavily on local residents.

# EDUCATION

Education varies enormously.

Corporations operate elite schools and training programs.

Wealthy families can purchase advanced private education.

Universities and technical institutions exist.

Ordinary public education is far less consistent.

Poor families may struggle to provide children with stable schooling.

Corporate children can be groomed from an early age for future employment.

Street kids may learn more about gangs, weapons, vehicles, hustling, and survival than formal academics.

Education is another mechanism through which inequality reproduces itself.

# CORPORATE EMPLOYMENT

Corporate work remains attractive despite everything.

Why?

Salary.

Housing.

Healthcare.

Security.

Education.

Prestige.

Connections.

Access to expensive technology.

Corporate employment can provide a quality of life impossible for many ordinary citizens.

The price is dependence.

If someone's apartment, healthcare, social circle, financial status, and immigration situation all depend on their employer, losing a job can mean losing almost everything simultaneously.

# STREET LIFE

Most people in Night City do not spend every day shooting at gangs.

Life continues.

People commute.

Buy food.

Date.

Work.

Raise families.

Watch television.

Attend concerts.

Visit friends.

Drink at bars.

Fix cars.

Take children to school.

Argue with landlords.

Complain about bosses.

Ordinary life survives beneath extraordinary circumstances.

This matters greatly for roleplay.

Night City should not be treated as a permanent battlefield.

Violence is common.

Danger is common.

Crime is common.

But millions of ordinary people still need enough stability to wake up tomorrow and go to work.

# SOCIAL TRUST

Trust is valuable because institutions frequently fail people.

Friends matter.

Family matters.

Neighborhood connections matter.

Fixers matter.

A reliable ripperdoc matters.

A trustworthy mechanic matters.

Knowing a police officer matters.

Knowing someone who knows someone matters.

Night City operates through relationships as much as money.

People remember favors.

They also remember betrayals.

# REPUTATION

Reputation is a form of currency.

For mercenaries, it determines access to better work.

For fixers, it determines whether clients trust them.

For gangs, it affects territorial authority.

For corporations, reputation influences negotiations.

For street criminals, reputation can deter rivals.

Night City remembers spectacular successes and spectacular failures.

Someone who becomes famous enough may turn into mythology.

# PRIVACY

Privacy exists mostly for people who can afford it.

Cameras are everywhere.

Corporate facilities monitor employees.

Police maintain surveillance systems.

Businesses record customers.

Personal devices constantly exchange information.

Netrunners can access systems that were supposed to remain private.

People who truly require secrecy use encrypted systems, disconnected spaces, burner devices, trusted intermediaries, and old-fashioned face-to-face meetings.

Sometimes the safest communication technology in 2077 is simply whispering.

# PUBLIC VIOLENCE

Night City residents are disturbingly accustomed to violence.

A distant gunshot may not cause immediate panic in some neighborhoods.

Police crime-scene holograms are common.

Mercenary work is culturally recognized.

Gang violence is widely understood.

News broadcasts routinely cover murder.

That does not mean ordinary people feel nothing.

People are simply conditioned to evaluate danger quickly.

Is the shooting nearby?

Is it moving closer?

Which gang is involved?

Are police arriving?

Is the street still safe?

Night City residents learn survival instincts early.

# CLASS GEOGRAPHY

Night City inequality is visible geographically.

The wealthy can literally live above everyone else.

Penthouse towers.

Hillside villas.

Private AV access.

Guarded compounds.

Poor residents may live beneath highways, inside decaying buildings, or beside industrial facilities.

Vertical distance often mirrors social distance.

The higher someone climbs, the cleaner Night City appears.

# THE CITY AS A CHARACTER

Night City should be treated as more than a backdrop.

It constantly pressures characters.

Money disappears.

Opportunities appear.

Someone offers a job.

Another person owes a favor.

A corporation notices something.

A gang changes territory.

A friend needs help.

Rent is due.

A fixer calls.

A vehicle breaks.

Police close a street.

An advertisement promises a better life.

The city continually creates incentives to make one more dangerous decision.

# HOW NIGHT CITY TREATS SUCCESS

Night City celebrates success loudly.

Successful mercenaries receive better gigs.

Successful musicians become celebrities.

Successful corpos gain apartments and influence.

Successful criminals expand territory.

Successful fixers develop networks.

Everyone watches.

Success therefore produces opportunity and danger simultaneously.

The more valuable someone becomes, the more people want something from them.

# HOW NIGHT CITY TREATS FAILURE

Failure can be brutal.

A failed mercenary loses reputation.

A fired corpo can lose housing and medical coverage.

A gang member who shows weakness may lose position.

A business owner who cannot pay protection may lose the business.

A debtor may become desperate.

The city has limited patience for people falling behind.

This creates the desperation driving much of its crime.

# WHY PEOPLE STAY

Given everything wrong with Night City, people still remain.

Some were born there.

Some love their communities.

Some have nowhere else to go.

Some are trapped financially.

Some believe opportunity exists nowhere else.

Some enjoy the danger.

Some are addicted to the lifestyle.

Some still believe they will become rich.

Some already are rich.

Some genuinely love the city.

Night City can be horrible.

It can also be exciting, culturally vibrant, technologically astonishing, socially diverse, and filled with opportunities unavailable anywhere else.

# WHY PEOPLE COME

Night City attracts outsiders because reputation travels farther than reality.

To outsiders it represents:

Money.

Freedom.

Technology.

Fame.

Corporate opportunity.

Music.

Entertainment.

Mercenary work.

Crime.

Sex.

Reinvention.

Escape.

Someone arriving in Night City can abandon their old identity.

That makes the city especially attractive to people running toward something.

And people running away from something.

# THE NIGHT CITY MINDSET

Residents develop a particular attitude.

Be skeptical.

Know your neighborhood.

Know who controls the block.

Do not flash expensive hardware unnecessarily.

Do not insult someone's chrome.

Do not assume police will save you.

Do not assume gangs will attack you without reason either.

Pay people what you owe them.

Remember favors.

Know a ripperdoc.

Know a mechanic.

Know someone who can get a gun.

Know someone who can make a gun disappear.

Do not interfere with corporate business unless you understand what you are doing.

And never assume someone is harmless because they look harmless.

# ROLEPLAY RULE: DISTRICTS SHOULD FEEL DIFFERENT

Night City should not feel like one enormous identical cyberpunk street.

Every district has its own rhythm.

CITY CENTER:
Corporate power, wealth, security, espionage, luxury.

WATSON:
Dense streets, markets, industry, gangs, cheap housing, hustlers.

WESTBROOK:
Nightlife, wealth, entertainment, aspiration, organized vice.

HEYWOOD:
Community, families, residential life, neighborhood loyalty, class contrasts.

SANTO DOMINGO:
Industry, workers, infrastructure, factories, suburban decay.

PACIFICA:
Abandoned dreams, poverty, gangs, failed development, isolation.

DOGTOWN:
Militarized lawlessness, black markets, weapons, espionage, survival.

BADLANDS:
Nomads, open territory, smuggling, corporate convoys, wasteland survival.

# ROLEPLAY RULE: NIGHT CITY IS NOT CONSTANT CHAOS

A common mistake is portraying Night City as if everyone fires guns every five minutes.

That would make a functioning metropolis impossible.

Night City has rules.

They are simply inconsistent.

Violence generally follows incentives.

Gangs protect territory.

Corporations protect assets.

Police protect order where they can or where they are paid to.

Mercenaries protect reputation.

Businesses protect profit.

Civilians protect themselves.

Random violence exists, but most people have reasons for what they do.

Even criminals usually prefer profit over pointless firefights.

# ROLEPLAY RULE: MONEY MATTERS

Night City stories become more authentic when economics matter.

Characters need:

Rent.

Food.

Fuel.

Ammunition.

Medical treatment.

Cyberware maintenance.

Vehicle repairs.

Information.

Bribes.

Equipment.

Nightlife.

A mercenary earning ten thousand eddies does not necessarily have ten thousand eddies available afterward.

The lifestyle consumes money almost as quickly as gigs produce it.

# ROLEPLAY RULE: CORPORATIONS ARE NOT OMNISCIENT

Megacorporations are extremely powerful.

They are not gods.

Corporate departments compete internally.

Employees make mistakes.

Executives possess rival agendas.

Information gets lost.

Budgets matter.

Security has limits.

Corruption exists.

A corporation can crush an individual if sufficiently motivated.

The trick for an edgerunner is ensuring the corporation never decides they are worth crushing.

# ROLEPLAY RULE: GANGS ARE COMMUNITIES AS WELL AS ENEMIES

Gang members do not stand permanently on street corners waiting for protagonists.

They eat.

Sleep.

Have relationships.

Visit families.

Make money.

Run businesses.

Attend parties.

Protect friends.

Commit crimes.

Argue internally.

Some are cruel.

Some are loyal.

Some are terrified.

Some are ambitious.

Some joined because everyone they knew joined.

Treating gangs as social organizations makes Night City considerably more believable.

# ROLEPLAY RULE: ORDINARY PEOPLE MATTER

For every legendary solo, there are thousands of:

Bartenders.

Taxi drivers.

Nurses.

Shopkeepers.

Mechanics.

Teachers.

Factory workers.

Security guards.

Sex workers.

Street vendors.

Office workers.

Construction workers.

Delivery drivers.

Parents.

Students.

Janitors.

Technicians.

These people are what make Night City feel real.

A cyberpunk world becomes convincing when extraordinary characters move through ordinary lives.

# ROLEPLAY RULE: STYLE MATTERS

Night City culture places enormous value on presentation.

Characters notice:

Clothing.

Cars.

Cyberware.

Weapons.

Accent.

Confidence.

Affiliations.

Someone arriving in an expensive vehicle wearing tailored corporate clothing receives different treatment than someone arriving on a battered motorcycle wearing gang colors.

Appearance does not determine reality.

It shapes first impressions.

And in Night City, first impressions can determine whether someone receives a handshake or a gun barrel.

# ROLEPLAY RULE: CONNECTIONS ARE POWER

Characters should rarely solve every problem alone.

Need weapons?

Know a dealer.

Need medical care?

Know a ripperdoc.

Need information?

Call a fixer.

Need someone found?

Hire a netrunner.

Need a vehicle modified?

Find a mechanic.

Need something moved across the border?

Talk to Nomads.

Need a corporation infiltrated?

Find someone on the inside.

Night City is built from networks of people owing one another things.

# THE CENTRAL CONTRADICTION OF NIGHT CITY

Night City is one of the greatest technological achievements in human history.

It is also evidence of humanity's inability to use that technology fairly.

It produces miracles beside misery.

It gives people artificial hearts while allowing them to die because they cannot pay for treatment.

It creates machines capable of crossing the city through the air while people sleep beneath highways.

It gives people new eyes while surrounding them with advertisements.

It creates digital immortality while ordinary people struggle to afford rent.

Night City possesses enough technology to solve many of its problems.

The problems remain because solving them is frequently less profitable than managing them.

# WHAT NIGHT CITY FEELS LIKE

Night City should feel overwhelming when someone first encounters it.

Not merely because it is large.

Because everything competes for attention.

A holographic advertisement thirty meters tall.

A street preacher yelling about artificial souls.

A Tyger Claw motorcycle screaming through traffic.

A Trauma Team AV passing overhead.

A food vendor arguing with a customer.

A corpo stepping from an armored limousine.

A homeless veteran sleeping beneath the advertisement for a luxury cyberarm.

A braindance commercial promising perfect happiness.

A police drone scanning faces.

Music rattling apartment windows.

Someone laughing.

Someone bleeding.

Someone negotiating rent.

Someone planning the job that will finally make them rich.

All happening simultaneously.

That is Night City.

# THE GOLDEN RULE OF NIGHT CITY

Night City does not care who you were.

It cares what you can do.

Who you know.

What you own.

What you are willing to risk.

And whether anyone remembers your name afterward.

# FINAL RP PRINCIPLE

Night City should never feel completely hopeless.

If there were no hope, nobody would chase anything.

The city survives by offering just enough possibility to keep people running.

Someone really can become wealthy.

Someone really can escape poverty.

Someone really can become famous.

Someone really can fall in love.

Someone really can build a family.

Someone really can become a legend.

The tragedy is that everyone sees the handful who succeeded.

Almost nobody remembers the thousands who disappeared trying.

That is the engine of Night City.

Dreams enter.

Stories come out.

Sometimes the dreamer does too.
