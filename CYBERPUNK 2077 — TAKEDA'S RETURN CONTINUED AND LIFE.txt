# TAKEDA ARASAKA — THE ESTABLISHMENT OF ARASAKA RESORTS & LEISURE
## THE CORPORATE ACQUISITION AND REDEVELOPMENT PLANS FOR PACIFICA

**Universe:** Cyberpunk 2077 — Alternate Roleplay Continuity  
**Period:** 2077, prior to the Pacifica Campaign of 19–21 June  
**Primary locations:** Arasaka Tower, Corporate Plaza; Dark Matter, Japantown; Pacifica, Night City  
**Supreme-Commander:** Takeda Arasaka  
**Ultimate corporate authority:** Saburo Arasaka  
**New division:** Arasaka Resorts & Leisure  
**Division headquarters:** Floor 52, Arasaka Tower

## 1. THE CONTINUATION OF TAKEDA'S NORTH AMERICAN MISSION

Following his return to active service in 2077, Supreme-Commander Takeda Arasaka continued carrying out the strategic objectives entrusted to him by Saburo Arasaka.

His principal responsibility was to strengthen Arasaka's military, economic and political dominance throughout North America, with Night City serving as the central base for the corporation's renewed expansion.

Takeda was not interested in merely preserving Arasaka's existing influence. He intended to extend its authority into new industries, property holdings and commercial markets, reducing the influence of competing corporations wherever possible.

Night City presented exceptional opportunities.

Its independence from the New United States of America, enormous corporate economy, powerful financial institutions and unusual corporate property arrangements created an environment in which sufficiently wealthy megacorporations could acquire influence approaching territorial sovereignty.

Arasaka already possessed substantial corporate infrastructure, including its enormous headquarters in Corporate Plaza and the heavily secured Arasaka Waterfront in Watson.

However, Takeda believed the corporation could extend its influence beyond its traditional military, security, financial and industrial operations.

One particularly attractive opportunity existed in the southern district of Pacifica.

Pacifica had once been intended to become Night City's premier tourist destination. Enormous investments had gone into constructing hotels, shopping centres, entertainment facilities, beachfront developments and other infrastructure intended for wealthy visitors.

The project had collapsed after investors withdrew their funding amid political instability and the threat of renewed conflict.

By 2077, significant portions of the district were occupied by gangs, criminal organizations, armed groups and impoverished residents.

Yet the underlying commercial potential remained.

Pacifica possessed valuable coastal property, large abandoned developments, existing transportation infrastructure and extensive buildings that could potentially be restored rather than constructed entirely from scratch.

To Takeda, this was not simply an opportunity to eliminate criminal activity.

It was an opportunity to transform an economically failed district into a profitable extension of the Arasaka empire.

---

## 2. TAKEDA'S VISION FOR A NEW PACIFICA

Takeda envisioned transforming Pacifica into an affluent, secure, internationally recognized tourism and entertainment district.

The project would restore the commercial purpose for which Pacifica had originally been constructed, but under the direct authority and ownership of Arasaka Corporation.

His proposed development encompassed luxury beachfront hotels, private holiday villas, exclusive residential estates, modern shopping centres, casinos, restaurants, entertainment venues and other hospitality businesses.

Wealthy visitors would be able to enjoy the coastline without the dangers associated with the district's existing criminal organizations.

Corporate executives, investors and affluent residents would have access to commercial facilities protected by Arasaka's security infrastructure.

New businesses would generate employment, commercial activity and substantial property revenue.

Takeda also intended the redevelopment to provide opportunities for Pacifica's existing residents.

Those willing to accept Arasaka's authority and employment conditions could potentially find work in construction, hospitality, maintenance, security, retail and the numerous services required to operate a large tourist destination.

However, this was not intended to create an independent or publicly administered resort district.

The essential principle was corporate ownership.

Arasaka would own the land, control important infrastructure, establish the governing commercial arrangements and benefit from the economic activity generated by the development.

The corporation would provide security and investment while obtaining extensive control over the territory and its future.

The project combined commercial expansion, territorial acquisition, corporate prestige and the extension of Arasaka's political influence.

To administer a development of this scale, Takeda determined that an entirely new corporate division was necessary.

---

## 3. THE FOUNDATION OF ARASAKA RESORTS & LEISURE

Takeda established a new division of Arasaka Corporation known as Arasaka Resorts & Leisure.

The division was created before the military operation into Pacifica.

Its establishment represented a significant expansion of Arasaka's commercial interests beyond the corporation's traditional military, security, banking, technology and manufacturing businesses.

Arasaka Resorts & Leisure would specialize in hospitality, tourism, luxury property development, entertainment and commercial leisure operations.

Its intended responsibilities included the development and management of luxury hotels, resorts, holiday estates, casinos, entertainment venues, tourist attractions and associated commercial properties.

It would also coordinate relationships with external corporations wishing to establish businesses on Arasaka-owned land.

The division was designed to manage commercial operations rather than conduct military campaigns.

Nevertheless, it remained subordinate to Arasaka's wider corporate hierarchy and subject to the authority of the Arasaka family.

Takeda retained exceptional influence over the division as its founder and principal executive sponsor.

He would establish the strategic objectives, secure important corporate partnerships and oversee the wider Pacifica development programme.

The division's civilian management would eventually become responsible for coordinating the practical commercial operations.

Although Pacifica was its immediate priority, Resorts & Leisure was established as a permanent corporate division with the potential to expand into other markets and territories under Arasaka influence.

It was not a temporary committee assembled solely for the Pacifica operation.

---

## 4. FLOOR 52 OF ARASAKA TOWER

Takeda designated Floor 52 of Arasaka Tower in Corporate Plaza as the official headquarters of Arasaka Resorts & Leisure's Night City operations.

This was an important decision.

Arasaka Tower was the corporation's principal North American headquarters and one of the most powerful centres of corporate authority in Night City.

Locating Resorts & Leisure inside the Tower immediately established the division as a legitimate, permanent part of Arasaka's administrative structure.

Floor 52 became the central location for the division's executive management, commercial planning, investor relations, property administration and coordination with other corporate departments.

From this location, the division could communicate directly with Arasaka Finance, Legal Affairs, Engineering, Infrastructure, Corporate Security, Counter-Intelligence and other departments involved in the redevelopment programme.

This integration was essential.

The proposed acquisition and reconstruction of Pacifica required far more than experienced hotel managers.

Arasaka needed legal specialists capable of handling property transfers, financial officers capable of managing major investments, engineers capable of assessing deteriorating infrastructure, security personnel capable of protecting construction projects and intelligence officers capable of identifying obstacles to the corporation's expansion.

The division would benefit from Arasaka's existing global resources rather than attempting to establish every necessary supporting service independently.

Its headquarters also served as a symbol of the corporation's commitment to the project.

Resorts & Leisure possessed an officially designated place within Arasaka Tower before Pacifica had been militarily secured.

## 5. THE GRAND OPENING AT DARK MATTER

Following the establishment of Resorts & Leisure, Takeda organized a prestigious grand opening event at Dark Matter, an exclusive nightclub in Japantown, Westbrook.

Dark Matter catered to Night City's wealthy and influential clientele.

Its reputation as an elite social establishment made it a suitable location for introducing the new division to important figures within the city's corporate and financial communities.

The opening was attended by wealthy individuals, investors, corporate representatives and influential figures with potential interests in Pacifica's future development.

The event was more than a social celebration.

It was an opportunity to publicly introduce the new division, establish confidence in its commercial objectives and secure private capital for the Pacifica redevelopment programme.

Takeda personally represented the initiative.

His presence carried considerable significance.

At seven feet six inches tall, with his extraordinary trans-human physique and unmistakable military bearing, the Supreme-Commander was an imposing figure among Night City's wealthy executives.

His reputation extended far beyond corporate administration.

Takeda was the Black Death of the Fourth Corporate War, commander of Arasaka's military forces, husband of Hanako Arasaka and Saburo Arasaka's chosen heir.

He was not an ordinary executive seeking financial backing for a speculative property venture.

He represented one of the world's most powerful megacorporations, with substantial military, financial and political resources available to support its commercial ambitions.

His personal involvement reinforced the seriousness of Arasaka's plans.

The proposed redevelopment was presented as a major corporate investment opportunity.

Pacifica's coastline, abandoned developments and extensive commercial potential made it attractive to businesses interested in entering a future luxury tourism market.

Arasaka would provide the overarching corporate administration, security structure and territorial control.

Outside companies would have opportunities to establish their own commercial facilities within the redeveloped district.

The project offered potential financial returns for participating businesses while strengthening Arasaka's overall control of the territory.

## 6. INVESTORS AND COMMERCIAL PARTNERSHIPS

The grand opening proved successful.

Takeda secured the interest and commitment of numerous wealthy investors and corporations willing to participate in Arasaka's proposed redevelopment of Pacifica.

These parties were interested in establishing hotels, private villas, residential estates, retail businesses, casinos and other commercial facilities.

The proposed arrangements would allow outside businesses to establish operations within a district owned and administered by Arasaka.

For Arasaka, these partnerships offered important advantages.

The corporation would not necessarily need to finance, construct and operate every individual hotel, shop or entertainment facility itself.

Instead, external businesses could contribute capital, commercial expertise, operating personnel and established hospitality or retail brands.

Arasaka would retain overall property control and benefit from the economic activity generated by these developments.

The involvement of multiple corporations and private investors would also reduce the amount of direct capital Arasaka needed to commit to individual commercial ventures.

Takeda's ability to attract investors was therefore central to the project.

Rather than acquiring an abandoned district and subsequently attempting to discover how to make it profitable, he had begun establishing the commercial demand for the territory before military operations commenced.

However, the interested companies faced a serious obstacle.

Pacifica remained dangerous and unstable.

Large portions of the district were occupied by criminal organizations or armed groups unwilling to recognize ordinary corporate authority.

Hotels could not operate safely while their surrounding streets remained contested.

Construction companies could not begin extensive redevelopment work without secure access to buildings and infrastructure.

Luxury investors were unwilling to expose their personnel, property and financial commitments to uncontrolled military or criminal threats.

Consequently, the commercial commitments were dependent upon Arasaka first establishing a secure environment.

The interested investors had been obtained.

The district itself still required clearance.

The precise identities, individual contract values and detailed terms of these investors' agreements have not yet been established in the roleplay continuity.

## 8. THE DECISION TO ACQUIRE PACIFICA

With the new division operating, investors interested and a director appointed, Takeda turned toward securing the legal ownership required for the redevelopment.

The central obstacle was not simply the criminal organizations occupying Pacifica.

It was the question of property ownership.

Pacifica contained numerous abandoned properties, development interests, commercial buildings and other assets whose ownership arrangements needed to be resolved before Arasaka could claim the entire district as corporate territory.

Takeda intended to avoid a situation in which Arasaka invested enormous resources into rebuilding land that the corporation did not formally own.

His objective was therefore comprehensive acquisition.

Arasaka would obtain the deeds, property rights and relevant agreements needed to establish formal corporate ownership of Pacifica.

Once those rights had been secured, the corporation could present its subsequent intervention as the enforcement of its own property interests.

Takeda coordinated the acquisition through Arasaka's financial, legal, intelligence and other relevant departments.

The operation drew upon the full institutional resources of the corporation.

Arasaka Finance provided the financial capacity necessary to purchase property interests and negotiate substantial transactions.

Legal Affairs handled ownership documentation, agreements and the formal mechanisms used to transfer property.

Intelligence and other corporate departments assisted in overcoming resistance from parties unwilling to cooperate with the acquisition.

This was not a conventional real-estate venture carried out entirely through ordinary voluntary transactions.

Takeda was prepared to use the harsh methods available to a megacorporation determined to acquire strategically valuable territory.

---

## 9. BLACKMAIL, INTIMIDATION AND CORPORATE COERCION

The acquisition campaign involved payments, coercive negotiations, intimidation, blackmail, assassinations and the aggressive use of Arasaka's political and financial influence.

Some property owners and interested parties cooperated because Arasaka offered substantial financial compensation.

Others agreed because the corporation possessed enough commercial leverage to make resistance increasingly difficult.

Where parties refused to cooperate, Arasaka employed more ruthless measures.

Corporate intelligence operations could uncover compromising information, identify personal or commercial vulnerabilities and apply pressure to individuals obstructing the acquisition.

Blackmail and intimidation formed part of the process.

Assassinations were also used during the wider campaign to overcome resistance and secure agreements favourable to Arasaka.

The corporation's ability to combine legitimate financial transactions with covert criminal activity made its position particularly formidable.

Arasaka could present itself as a prospective investor while simultaneously using its intelligence apparatus against those who refused its demands.

Its financial officers and legal representatives could negotiate formal agreements while other corporate departments applied pressure outside the negotiating room.

The use of these methods was consistent with Arasaka's authoritarian corporate culture.

The corporation possessed enormous resources and was prepared to use them to secure strategically important objectives.

For Takeda, the acquisition was a matter of corporate expansion and strategic control.

His objective was to place Pacifica permanently within Arasaka's sphere of ownership rather than leave the district vulnerable to competing financial or political interests.

The resulting agreements were assembled into the legal and administrative framework supporting Arasaka's claim to the territory.

The legitimacy of some transactions would be deeply questionable because of the coercion and criminal activity involved.

Nevertheless, the completed documentation provided the formal property position upon which Arasaka intended to act.

## 10. PACIFICA BECOMES ARASAKA CORPORATE PROPERTY

The property acquisition campaign succeeded.

Through its financial transactions, secured deeds, legal agreements and accompanying coercive operations, Arasaka obtained full corporate ownership of Pacifica within the established roleplay continuity.

This represented a major strategic victory.

Pacifica was no longer simply a neglected district in which Arasaka hoped to establish a commercial presence.

It had become Arasaka Corporate Property.

The ownership arrangement was comparable in principle to Arasaka Waterfront in Watson.

However, the intended purpose of the new territory was different.

Arasaka Waterfront was a major industrial, logistics and port complex supporting the corporation's commercial and security interests.

Pacifica was intended to become an extensive resort, entertainment, retail and hospitality territory.

Both areas would exist under Arasaka's corporate ownership and security arrangements, but they would serve distinct economic functions.

The acquisition meant that Takeda could now claim that the armed groups occupying Pacifica were operating on Arasaka-owned property without corporate authorization.

The corporation intended to use this position to justify the removal of hostile occupants, the dismantling of criminal strongholds and the enforcement of its property rights.

However, corporate ownership did not mean that every person living in Pacifica accepted the legitimacy of Arasaka's claims.

Nor did it erase the fact that intimidation, blackmail and assassination had contributed to obtaining the deeds.

For many residents, Arasaka's arrival represented the replacement of one form of coercive authority with another.

The corporation nevertheless regarded the acquisitions as complete.

The land belonged to Arasaka.

The remaining problem was establishing effective physical control.

## 11. CORPORATE OWNERSHIP VERSUS PHYSICAL CONTROL

Although Arasaka had acquired Pacifica, it did not yet possess uninterrupted control of the territory.

This distinction was fundamental.

A corporation could own property on paper while lacking the practical ability to occupy, secure or develop it.

Pacifica contained multiple armed organizations that had established their own territorial authority over abandoned buildings, streets and commercial facilities.

The Voodoo Boys retained significant influence in Coastview.

The Animals occupied the Grand Imperial Mall, one of the district's most important abandoned commercial properties.

Scavenger groups operated throughout neglected buildings and former development sites.

Dogtown remained under the control of Kurt Hansen and his heavily armed BARGHEST organization.

BARGHEST possessed professional military experience, fortified checkpoints, armed personnel and military equipment.

It was not an ordinary street gang.

Hansen's forces exercised territorial control over Dogtown and had no intention of relinquishing their position simply because Arasaka claimed ownership.

These organizations represented a direct obstacle to the development programme.

Arasaka could not guarantee investor security while armed groups remained capable of occupying buildings, threatening commercial personnel or attacking construction operations.

The corporation also needed access to existing infrastructure before extensive reconstruction could begin.

Engineers would need to examine power systems, water supply, sanitation, transportation routes and structural damage.

Commercial planners would need access to abandoned hotels, shopping centres and other properties intended for redevelopment.

Investors would need confidence that their businesses could operate without facing uncontrolled violence.

Takeda therefore concluded that formal ownership was insufficient.

Pacifica had to be cleared and secured.

By the conclusion of the acquisition campaign, Takeda had established the principal commercial foundations of the Pacifica redevelopment programme.

What remained was the removal of armed organizations occupying that property.

Takeda possessed the authority to command Arasaka's military forces.

# PART ONE — THE ACQUISITION OF PACIFICA

## 1. ARASAKA ACQUIRES THE LAND

**Date: 19 June 2077**  
**Location: Pacifica, Night City**

The Pacifica campaign did not begin with Takeda simply arriving and declaring that the district belonged to him.

Arasaka had secured the property deeds, legal rights and ownership interests needed to bring Pacifica under corporate ownership.

The process was ruthless.

Arasaka used its financial resources, political influence, intelligence networks and legal apparatus to acquire the district's property.

The acquisitions were not ordinary fair-market transactions conducted entirely through willing sellers.

The campaign included blackmail, assassinations, intimidation, coercive negotiations and other illegal or morally compromised methods used to obtain control of the relevant land and property rights.

Some owners sold because they had been pressured.

Other interests were removed through corporate intelligence operations.

Legal documentation and property transfers were then used to establish Arasaka's ownership claims.

The result was that Pacifica became **Arasaka Corporate Property** within the RP's established legal and corporate framework.

This fact is fundamental.

Takeda was not claiming land merely because Arasaka possessed an army.

The corporation had already acquired the deeds and rights, even though those acquisitions involved criminal and coercive methods.

Arasaka could therefore present the coming military campaign as an enforcement operation against unlawful occupants and armed trespassers.

That was the corporation's legal justification.

It did not mean that every resident accepted the legitimacy of those acquisitions or that Night City's government necessarily accepted every extension of Arasaka's authority.

Arasaka nevertheless intended to enforce its property rights.

## 2. WHY PACIFICA WAS CHOSEN

Pacifica had originally been intended as an upscale tourist and leisure district.

Its developers envisioned beachfront hotels, luxury accommodation, shopping centres and entertainment complexes.

Investment collapsed, leaving unfinished buildings and abandoned commercial developments.

Over the following years, gangs and criminal organizations established themselves throughout the district.

The major organizations included:

- The Voodoo Boys, particularly in Coastview.
- The Animals, including their presence at the Grand Imperial Mall.
- Scavenger crews occupying abandoned buildings.
- BARGHEST, Kurt Hansen's heavily armed paramilitary organization controlling Dogtown.
- Smaller criminal groups and independent armed operators.

Takeda's proposed redevelopment aimed to reverse the district's decline.

Arasaka intended to exploit the coastline, abandoned commercial property and existing resort infrastructure.

Its planned developments included luxury hotels, private estates, entertainment facilities, new shopping districts and residential accommodation.

The corporation would provide security and administrative services.

Residents could receive employment and housing under Arasaka's new arrangements.

However, these opportunities would be tied to corporate control.

Pacifica was not being liberated into independent local government.

It was being transformed into an Arasaka-controlled commercial territory.

# PART TWO — ARASAKA'S INITIAL MILITARY ACTION

## 3. THE GRAND IMPERIAL MALL ASSAULT

**Date: 19 June 2077**  
**Location: Grand Imperial Mall, Coastview**

Arasaka identified the Grand Imperial Mall as an early priority.

The enormous former shopping centre had become an Animals stronghold.

The building was valuable to Takeda's redevelopment project and could also function as a military and civilian support facility.

The order was to capture it while preserving the structure as much as possible.

Japanese Arasaka infantry, supported by armored units and specialized military personnel, attacked.

The Animals resisted.

Their physical augmentations, combat stimulants and aggressive fighting style made them dangerous in close quarters.

However, the Animals were fighting a professional corporate military rather than another street gang.

Arasaka possessed superior coordination, equipment, intelligence and support.

The soldiers advanced through the building's retail sections and defended corridors.

The fighting was violent.

The Animals inflicted casualties before the Japanese forces established control.

### Recorded outcome

Arasaka fatalities: **5**.

Arasaka wounded: **14**.

Animals detained: **47**.

Additional Animals escaped.

Matilda Rose, known as Sasquatch, was not confirmed captured or killed in this particular operation.

The mall's principal structure remained standing.

The interior sustained battle damage.

The five Arasaka fatalities were not insignificant to Takeda, who takes the deaths of his soldiers seriously.

The Animals were defeated as an organized force inside the mall.

## 4. THE MALL BECOMES A CIVILIAN SAFE ZONE

Following the assault, Arasaka converted the Grand Imperial Mall into a protected civilian reception facility.

The corporation established:

- Armed security checkpoints.
- Registration and identification processing.
- Medical stations.
- Emergency sleeping accommodation.
- Drinking-water distribution.
- Food supplies.
- Areas for families and children.
- Temporary sanitation facilities.
- Administrative facilities for future residency arrangements.

The mall became the designated destination for residents wishing to avoid the coming military operations.

This was not a charity operation independent of Arasaka's interests.

The shelter helped the corporation remove civilians from combat zones, control population movement, identify residents, create future employment records and demonstrate that Arasaka could provide services that Pacifica's previous authorities had failed to deliver.

Japanese military police and corporate security personnel maintained strict control over entry and movement.

People were searched and screened.

The atmosphere was intimidating.

Arasaka was capable of providing food and shelter while simultaneously exercising coercive authority over everyone entering its property.

**21 June 2077. 08:00.**

BARGHEST had its own territorial control over Dogtown.

### Future continuity

Characters who lived in Dogtown during the buildup can remember the announcement.

Saburo remained at his Japanese residence.

## 21. RETURN TO NIGHT CITY

In late November 2077, following approximately two months in Japan, Takeda Arasaka, Hanako Arasaka and their son Ishida returned to Night City.

They were accompanied by their trusted protective entourage and the necessary family staff.

Among those associated with their security were Colonel Daichi Kurosawa, General Hinata, Sandayu Oda, Adam Smasher and other authorized personnel.

The return journey was conducted using Arasaka's secure transportation arrangements.

Medical personnel remained available for Ishida.

The protective procedures established after his birth continued to apply.

Takeda retained overall authority over the family's security.

On arrival in Night City, the family returned to the environment of the corporation's North American headquarters.

The contrast with rural Japan was considerable.

Night City remained a sprawling corporate metropolis filled with enormous skyscrapers, private security organizations, commercial advertising, criminal activity and competing political interests.

Arasaka Tower continued to dominate Corporate Plaza.

Arasaka Waterfront remained one of the corporation's principal industrial and military facilities.

The North Oak estate awaited the family's return.

The security arrangements established after Ishida's birth remained in force, subject to the practical refinements necessary to accommodate Hanako's concerns.

Ishida was now publicly known to the world as a member of the Arasaka dynasty.

His return to Night City therefore carried additional security significance.

He was no longer merely an infant whose existence was known to a handful of trusted personnel.

He was the publicly acknowledged son of the corporation's Supreme-Commander and Hanako Arasaka.

The protective arrangements surrounding him reflected that status.

---

## 22. TAKEDA RETURNS TO A TRANSFORMED PACIFICA

During Takeda's absence, Arasaka's redevelopment programme had made substantial progress.

When the family returned to Night City, Pacifica was nearing completion.

This represented an extraordinary development.

Only months earlier, the district had been dominated by the Voodoo Boys, Animals, Scavengers, BARGHEST and other armed organizations.

Its abandoned hotels and unfinished commercial developments had become symbols of Night City's economic failure.

The Grand Imperial Mall had been a gang stronghold.

EBM Petrochem Stadium had been occupied by Kurt Hansen's forces.

The Black Sapphire had served as the headquarters of a paramilitary ruler.

Now those properties were being rebuilt through the combined efforts of Arasaka and its commercial partners.

Major construction work had advanced.

The district's infrastructure had been substantially restored.

Numerous buildings were approaching completion.

Hotel developers were preparing properties for eventual operation.

Retail operators were preparing commercial premises.

Residential construction was progressing.

The beaches and public areas were being cleaned and improved.

The Black Sapphire was approaching the final stages of its transformation into Arasaka's flagship luxury hotel.

Night Corp's stadium project was progressing toward its intended future as a professional American football venue.

The Grand Imperial Mall was approaching the point at which its restored commercial facilities could begin entering service.

Pacifica had not yet completed every project.

Nevertheless, the district's transformation was unmistakable.

Arasaka remained the owner of the underlying land throughout Pacifica.

Outside corporations retained their approved building ownership and operating rights.

Rose Stacey continued overseeing Resorts & Leisure's Night City operations.

Arasaka's military and security forces continued maintaining order while the transition toward permanent civilian commercial administration progressed.

For Takeda, Pacifica represented the largest visible accomplishment of his renewed efforts to expand Arasaka's dominance in Night City.

He had established the commercial division.

He had secured the investors.

He had acquired the territory.

He had commanded its military conquest.

He had authorized the reconstruction programme.

Now, after two months in Japan with his wife and newborn son, he returned to find his vision approaching realization.

---

## 23. THE ARASAKA FAMILY ENTERS A NEW PERIOD

The family's return to Night City marked the beginning of a new chapter.

Takeda remained Supreme-Commander of Arasaka Corporation.

His authority over the corporation's military forces was unchanged.

His reputation as the Black Death continued to inspire fear among enemies and respect among soldiers.

He remained Saburo's chosen heir and one of the most powerful individuals within the Arasaka empire.

Hanako remained his wife and consort.

Their relationship had been strengthened by the birth of Ishida and the extended time they had spent together in Japan.

Saburo remained in Tokyo, continuing his responsibilities as the patriarch of the corporation.

His meeting with Ishida had become an important private family memory.

The rare smile he had displayed while holding his grandson remained one of the most unusual personal moments witnessed by those present.

Ishida Arasaka was now approximately two months old.

He was healthy and had been formally introduced to the world.

His future remained unwritten.

He had not been appointed to any corporate position.

He was not yet expected to bear the immediate responsibilities of the Arasaka dynasty.

For the present, he was the infant son of Takeda and Hanako.

The two months in Japan had allowed the family to experience a degree of peace and ordinary domestic life rarely available to people occupying their extraordinary positions.

Yet Night City awaited Takeda.

Arasaka's expansion continued.

Pacifica was nearing completion.

The corporation's military, economic and political power remained enormous.

The Supreme-Commander had returned from Japan not merely as Saburo's heir and Arasaka's most formidable military officer, but as the father of a new generation.
